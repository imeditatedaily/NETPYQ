# Nutrition I: Food Components, Carbohydrates, Proteins, Fats, Fibre and Water

> **At a glance** — proximate principles = **proteins, fats, carbohydrates** · micronutrients = **vitamins and minerals** · body-building foods = **pulses, meat** · vegetarian protein = **pulses + cereals** · most protein: **soybean** · richest milk in protein: **buffalo** · RDA protein: **1 g/kg** (older ICMR) → **0.83 g/kg** (ICMR-NIN 2020) · **22** proteinogenic amino acids (20 standard) · **arginine** is not essential for adults · ketogenic: **leucine** (and lysine) · PUFA-rich: **mustard, fish oil, sesame** · highest-fibre cereal: **ragi** · protein deficiency in children: **marasmus, kwashiorkor** · discoverers: protein–**Mulder**, vitamins–**Funk**, essential fatty acids–**Burr and Burr**

Syllabus: "Diet and nutrition: components of food; macronutrients and micronutrients." Nutrition (with vitamins and energy) has given over 55 questions — more than any other block of Unit 6.

## 1. Food, nutrients and their classification

**Nutrition** — the process by which the body takes in and uses food for growth, energy, repair and protection. **Nutrients** — chemical substances in food that do this. **Diet** — the food a person habitually eats.

**Six classes of nutrients**: **carbohydrates, proteins, fats** (macronutrients), **vitamins, minerals** (micronutrients), and **water** (plus dietary fibre).

- **Proximate principles** of food — the three energy-giving nutrients present in large amounts: **proteins, fats and carbohydrates**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q35]]
- **Micronutrients** — **vitamins and minerals**, needed in milligrams or micrograms. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q56]] Minerals are micronutrients *because* they are essential but needed only in small amounts. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q28]]

**Classification by function** (ICMR's classic grouping):

| Group | Function | Foods |
|---|---|---|
| **Energy-giving** | fuel | cereals, millets, roots and tubers, sugar, fats and oils |
| **Body-building** | growth and repair (protein) | **pulses**, **meat**, fish, eggs, milk, nuts |
| **Protective** | regulate body processes, prevent disease (vitamins, minerals) | fruits, vegetables (especially green leafy), milk |

**Body-building foods** among fats, pulses, meat, oils: **pulses and meat**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q110]]

**Who discovered what** (asked as a match): **proteins → G. J. Mulder** (named "protein", 1838; Greek *proteios*, "of first rank"); **vitamins → Casimir Funk** ("vitamine", 1912); **iodine → "Candal"** (E. C. **Kendall** isolated the iodine hormone thyroxine, 1914; the element itself was found by Courtois, 1811); **essential fatty acids → George and Mildred Burr** (1929). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024JUN-P2-Q079]]

**Matching nutrients with examples**: **fat → linolenic acid**; **monosaccharide → fructose**; **disaccharide → sucrose**; **protein → tryptophan** (an amino acid). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q076]]

## 2. Carbohydrates

Compounds of C, H and O (CH₂O)ₙ; the body's main and cheapest fuel: **4 kcal/g**; should give about **50–60%** of energy (ICMR-NIN 2024 advises not more than ~45% from cereals, the rest from millets, pulses, vegetables, fruits).

| Class | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Monosaccharides** (one unit) | **glucose** (blood sugar, dextrose), **fructose** (fruit sugar, sweetest), **galactose** | absorbed directly |
| **Disaccharides** (two units) | **sucrose** = glucose + fructose (cane sugar); **lactose** = glucose + galactose (milk sugar); **maltose** = glucose + glucose | split by sucrase, lactase, maltase |
| Oligosaccharides | raffinose, stachyose (in pulses: cause flatulence) | |
| **Polysaccharides** (many units) | **starch** (amylose + amylopectin: cereals, tubers), **glycogen** ("animal starch" in liver and muscle), **cellulose** (plant fibre: indigestible to humans) | |

Order **mono → di → poly**: **fructose, galactose → sucrose, lactose → starch**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-Q79]]

**Dietary fibre** (roughage) — plant material not digested by human enzymes:
- **soluble** (pectin, gums, β-glucan in oats and barley, psyllium): lowers cholesterol and blood-sugar rise;
- **insoluble** (cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin: whole grains, bran): adds bulk, prevents constipation.

Adults need roughly **30–40 g a day**. Among cereals, **ragi (finger millet)** has the **maximum fibre** (~11 g/100 g, against ~2–3 g in polished rice). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q21]] Fibre lowers the glycaemic index of a meal, feeds gut bacteria (prebiotic), and protects against diabetes, heart disease and colon cancer. [[LIKELY]]

## 3. Proteins and amino acids

Proteins are chains of **amino acids** (C, H, O, **N**, sometimes S). **4 kcal/g**. Functions: growth and **repair** of tissues, enzymes, hormones (insulin), antibodies, transport (haemoglobin, albumin), muscle contraction (actin, myosin), clotting, energy when needed.

**How many amino acids?** — **20 standard** amino acids; with **selenocysteine** (21st) and **pyrrolysine** (22nd) there are **22 proteinogenic** amino acids. [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S28]]

**Essential amino acids** (the body cannot make them) — **9** for adults: **histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine**. **Arginine** is *conditionally* (semi-) essential — needed in the diet in infancy and illness — so it is **not** an essential amino acid for adults. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q070]] (Mnemonic for the 10 including arginine: **PVT TIM HALL**.)

**Glucogenic vs ketogenic**: purely **ketogenic** (broken down to acetyl-CoA / acetoacetate) — **leucine and lysine**; both ketogenic and glucogenic — **isoleucine, phenylalanine, tyrosine**, tryptophan, threonine; the rest glucogenic. So the ketogenic pairs among the options were **leucine–isoleucine** and **tyrosine–phenylalanine** (ovovitellin and excelsin are whole proteins; chymotrypsin is an enzyme). [[PYQ: YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q53]]

**Protein quality** [[LIKELY]]: **biological value** (egg = reference protein, BV ~94–100), net protein utilisation (NPU), PDCAAS. **Limiting amino acid**: cereals are low in **lysine**, pulses in **methionine** → a **cereal + pulse** combination (dal–rice, khichdi, idli) complements them — **mutual supplementation**. So the main protein sources of a vegetarian diet are **pulses and cereals**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q51]]

**Protein content of foods** (per 100 g, Indian food tables):
- **Soybean ~38–43 g** (the highest of common foods) [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q13]]; groundnut ~25 g; pulses (dals) ~20–25 g; meat, fish ~18–22 g; egg ~13 g; cereals 6–12 g; milk ~3–4 g.
- **Milk** (per 100 mL): **buffalo ~4.3 g** protein (and ~6.5 g fat) — the richest; goat ~3.3 g; cow ~3.2 g (fat ~4.1 g); human ~1.1 g. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q090]]

**Requirement** — the older ICMR recommendation was **1 g protein per kg body weight per day** for an adult [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q07]]; ICMR-NIN **2020** lowered it to **0.83 g/kg/day** (≈ 54 g for a 65-kg man, 46 g for a 55-kg woman); more in pregnancy, lactation, growth and illness. [[LIKELY]] Protein should give ~10–15% of energy.

## 4. Fats (lipids) and oils

Fats give **9 kcal/g** (the most concentrated energy), carry **fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)**, supply **essential fatty acids**, make cell membranes and hormones, insulate and cushion organs. Should give ~20–30% of energy.

| Fatty acid type | Structure | Sources | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Saturated (SFA)** | no double bonds; solid at room temperature | **ghee, butter**, coconut oil, palm oil, meat fat | raises LDL cholesterol |
| **Monounsaturated (MUFA)** | one double bond (oleic acid) | olive, **groundnut**, **mustard**, rice-bran, sesame oil | heart-friendly |
| **Polyunsaturated (PUFA)** — **n-6** | linoleic acid | sunflower, safflower, corn, soybean, **sesame** oil | lowers LDL |
| **PUFA — n-3** (omega-3) | α-linolenic acid (plant); **EPA and DHA** (marine) | **mustard oil**, flaxseed (alsi), walnuts, soybean; **fish oil** | anti-inflammatory, heart and brain health |
| **Trans fats** | hydrogenated | **vanaspati**, bakery and fried snacks | most harmful; keep < 1% of energy |

Foods with a high percentage of **PUFA** among mustard oil, fish oil, ghee, butter and sesame oil: **mustard oil, fish oil and sesame oil** (ghee and butter are saturated). [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q114]]

**Essential fatty acids**: **linoleic** (n-6) and **α-linolenic** (n-3) acids — Burr and Burr showed rats on a fat-free diet developed deficiency (scaly skin, poor growth). ICMR advises using a **combination of oils** (e.g. groundnut + mustard) to balance n-6 and n-3. [[LIKELY]]

**Fat in nuts** (per 100 g; Indian food tables): **walnut ~64.5 g**, **almond ~58.9 g**, **cashew ~46.9 g**, **groundnut ~40.1 g**. The keyed order was almond, walnut, cashew, groundnut — the true order puts walnut first; cashew and groundnut are last in both. [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q132]]

**Cholesterol** — made by the liver; needed for membranes, steroid hormones, vitamin D and bile; **LDL** ("bad") deposits in arteries, **HDL** ("good") removes it. Plant foods contain no cholesterol.

## 5. Water, malnutrition and protein-energy deficiency

**Water** — ~**60%** of adult body weight (men ~60%, women ~50–55%, infants ~75%); **two-thirds intracellular**, one-third extracellular (plasma and interstitial fluid). Functions: solvent, transport, temperature regulation (sweat), lubrication, digestion and excretion. Need ~2–3 L a day (more in heat and exercise); thirst is controlled by the hypothalamus and ADH. [[LIKELY]]

**Malnutrition** (कुपोषण) — any imbalance of nutrients. Its forms are **over-nutrition**, **under-nutrition** and **imbalanced nutrition** (proper and balanced nutrition are not malnutrition). [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S26]] WHO groups it as **undernutrition** (wasting = low weight-for-height, stunting = low height-for-age, underweight = low weight-for-age), **micronutrient-related malnutrition**, and **overweight/obesity** with diet-related non-communicable diseases.

**Protein-energy malnutrition (PEM)** in childhood — **marasmus** and **kwashiorkor**: [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q102]]

| | **Marasmus** | **Kwashiorkor** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | severe lack of **both energy and protein** | mainly **protein** lack, often with enough calories (starchy diet after weaning) |
| Age | usually under 1 year | 1–4 years (term from Ghana's Ga language, "disease of the deposed child"; Cicely Williams, 1933) |
| Oedema | **absent** | **present** (pitting oedema, "moon face") |
| Look | extreme wasting, "skin and bones", **old-man face** | **flaky-paint dermatosis**, depigmented sparse hair (**flag sign**), fatty enlarged liver, apathy |
| Appetite | good | poor |

Rickets (vitamin D), keratomalacia and xerophthalmia (vitamin A) are **vitamin** deficiencies, not protein deficiencies. The mixed form is **marasmic kwashiorkor**. [[LIKELY]] Treatment and prevention: breastfeeding, timely complementary feeding, ICDS and the mid-day meal (PM POSHAN) scheme, POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018).

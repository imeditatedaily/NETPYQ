# Cell, Tissues, Blood and Immunity

> **At a glance** — cell discovered by **Robert Hooke, 1665** · membrane = **phospholipids + globular proteins + carbohydrates** (fluid mosaic model) · **ribosome** = protein synthesis · **lysosome** = "suicide bag" · Na⁺–K⁺ pump: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in · resting potential ≈ −70 mV · 4 tissues: epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous · **blood is a connective tissue**; **plasma ≈ 55%** · antigen → **antibodies** · innate = genetically determined; adaptive = specific; B cells = antibodies; T cells = cell-mediated · lymphoid tissues: tonsils, spleen, thymus, lymph nodes (not adrenal or thyroid)

Syllabus: "Cell physiology: structure and function of cell organelles; membrane transport; bioelectric potentials", plus the immune system.

## 1. The cell and its organelles

**History:**
- **Robert Hooke (1665)** first saw and named "cells" in a thin slice of cork (*Micrographia*). [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S21]]
- Antonie van **Leeuwenhoek** (1674) first saw living cells (protozoa, bacteria, sperm).
- Robert **Brown** (1831) discovered the nucleus.
- **Cell theory**: Matthias **Schleiden** (plants, 1838) and Theodor **Schwann** (animals, 1839); Rudolf **Virchow** (1855): *omnis cellula e cellula* ("every cell from a cell").

**Cell membrane (plasma membrane)** — the **fluid mosaic model** (Singer and Nicolson, 1972): a **phospholipid bilayer** (hydrophilic heads outside, hydrophobic tails inside) with **globular proteins** floating in it (**integral** proteins spanning it: channels, carriers, receptors; **peripheral** proteins on the surface), **cholesterol** for stability, and **carbohydrates** attached to proteins and lipids (**glycocalyx**: cell recognition, blood-group antigens). So an animal cell membrane contains **phospholipids and globular proteins** (and carbohydrates); calcium is not a structural component. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q50]] Thickness ~7.5–10 nm; **selectively permeable**.

| Organelle | Structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Nucleus** | double membrane with pores; chromatin (DNA + histones); **nucleolus** | control centre; DNA replication and transcription; nucleolus makes ribosomal RNA |
| **Ribosomes** | RNA + protein; 80S (60S + 40S) in the cytoplasm, 70S in mitochondria; free or on rough ER (George Palade, 1955) | **protein synthesis** (translation) [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q12]] |
| **Rough ER** | membranes studded with ribosomes | makes proteins for export and membranes |
| **Smooth ER** | no ribosomes | lipid and **steroid** synthesis, detoxification (liver), **Ca²⁺ store** (the sarcoplasmic reticulum in muscle) |
| **Golgi apparatus** (Camillo Golgi, 1898) | stacked flat sacs | modifies, sorts, **packages** proteins; makes lysosomes; secretion |
| **Lysosomes** (Christian de Duve, 1955) | membrane sacs of ~50 **hydrolytic enzymes** at acid pH (~5) | intracellular digestion, **autophagy** (old organelles), **autolysis**: if they burst, the cell digests itself — the "**suicide bags**" (आत्महत्या की थैली) [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q08]] |
| **Peroxisomes** | small sacs with **catalase** | break down H₂O₂ and very-long-chain fatty acids; detoxify alcohol |
| **Mitochondria** | double membrane; inner folds = **cristae**; matrix with own **DNA** (inherited from the mother) | "**powerhouse**": aerobic respiration, Krebs cycle (matrix), electron transport chain and **ATP** synthesis (inner membrane) |
| **Centrosome** (two centrioles, 9 triplets) | near nucleus | organises the **spindle** in cell division |
| **Cytoskeleton** | microfilaments (**actin**), intermediate filaments, **microtubules** (tubulin) | shape, movement, transport |
| Cilia and flagella | 9 + 2 microtubules | movement (cilia sweep mucus in the airways; sperm flagellum) |

**Cell division** — **mitosis** (two identical diploid cells, for growth and repair: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase) and **meiosis** (four haploid gametes; crossing over). Human cells: **46 chromosomes** (23 pairs; 22 autosome pairs + XX or XY). DNA double helix: Watson and Crick, 1953.

## 2. Membrane transport and bioelectric potentials

| Type | Energy | How | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple diffusion** | passive | down the concentration gradient through the lipid bilayer | O₂, CO₂, alcohol, fat-soluble substances |
| **Facilitated diffusion** | passive | down the gradient via **channel** or **carrier** proteins | **glucose** via GLUT transporters; ions through channels |
| **Osmosis** | passive | movement of **water** across a semipermeable membrane from low to high solute concentration (through **aquaporins**) | — |
| **Primary active transport** | ATP | against the gradient by a pump | **Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase**: **3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in** per ATP (Jens Skou, 1957) |
| **Secondary active transport** | uses the Na⁺ gradient | co-transport (symport) or counter-transport (antiport) | **Na⁺–glucose co-transport (SGLT)** in the gut — the basis of **ORS** |
| **Endocytosis** | ATP | membrane engulfs material | **phagocytosis** ("cell eating": neutrophils, macrophages), **pinocytosis** ("cell drinking"), receptor-mediated |
| **Exocytosis** | ATP | vesicles fuse with the membrane | hormone and neurotransmitter release |

**Tonicity:** **isotonic** (0.9% NaCl, "normal saline": no change), **hypotonic** (water enters: RBCs swell and burst — haemolysis), **hypertonic** (water leaves: RBCs shrink — crenation).

**Bioelectric potentials** [[LIKELY]]:
- **Resting membrane potential** (RMP): inside negative — about **−70 mV** in neurons, **−90 mV** in skeletal muscle; caused mainly by **K⁺ leaking out** through leak channels, plus the Na⁺–K⁺ pump.
- **Action potential**: a stimulus reaching **threshold** (~ −55 mV) opens voltage-gated **Na⁺ channels** → Na⁺ rushes in → **depolarisation** to about +30/+35 mV → Na⁺ channels close, **K⁺ channels** open, K⁺ leaves → **repolarisation** → brief **hyperpolarisation** → pump restores ions.
- **All-or-none law**: once threshold is reached, the action potential is full size.
- **Refractory period**: **absolute** (no new impulse possible) and **relative** (only a stronger stimulus works).
- Worked out by **Hodgkin and Huxley** on the squid giant axon (1952; Nobel 1963).

## 3. Tissues

A **tissue** is a group of similar cells doing a common function. There are **four** basic tissues:

| Tissue | Types and where | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Epithelial** (उपकला) | simple squamous (alveoli, blood-vessel lining = endothelium), simple cuboidal (kidney tubules, glands), simple columnar (stomach, intestine), ciliated (trachea), stratified squamous (skin — keratinised; mouth, oesophagus), transitional (urinary bladder), glandular | **covers body surfaces and lines inner cavities**; protection, absorption, secretion |
| **Connective** (संयोजी) — the most abundant | areolar (loose), adipose (fat), **dense regular** (**tendons**: muscle to bone; **ligaments**: bone to bone), cartilage (hyaline, elastic, fibro-), **bone**, and the fluid connective tissues **blood and lymph** | supports, binds, protects, transports |
| **Muscular** | skeletal, cardiac, smooth (see *Musculoskeletal System*) | movement |
| **Nervous** | neurons and neuroglia | conducts impulses, coordination |

**Blood is a connective tissue** (a fluid connective tissue: cells in a liquid matrix, plasma). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q26]]

On body tissues: **epithelial tissues cover the body surface and inner cavities**, and **blood and lymph are connective tissues** with a distinctive (fluid) matrix. Tendons connect muscle to **bone** (ligaments connect bone to bone), and the heart has **cardiac**, not smooth, muscle. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q114]]

**Organs and their functional units:** neuron → **brain**; cornea → **eye**; nephron → **kidney**; alveoli → **lungs**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q82]]

## 4. Blood

**Volume**: about **5–6 litres** in an adult (7–8% of body weight). pH 7.35–7.45.

**Composition:** **plasma ≈ 55%** and formed elements ≈ 45% (the **haematocrit**, packed cell volume). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q22]] Plasma is ~91% water, ~7% proteins (**albumin** — osmotic pressure; **globulins** — antibodies, transport; **fibrinogen** — clotting), plus nutrients, salts, hormones and wastes. Serum = plasma minus clotting factors.

| Formed element | Count | Life | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RBC** (erythrocytes) | 4.5–5.5 million/µL (men), ~4–5 million (women) | **120 days**; destroyed in the spleen ("graveyard of RBCs") | biconcave, **no nucleus**; **haemoglobin** carries O₂ (13.5–17.5 g/dL men, 12–15.5 women) |
| **WBC** (leucocytes) | 4,000–11,000/µL | hours to years | defence |
| — neutrophils | 50–70% (most numerous) | | first phagocytes at infection |
| — lymphocytes | 20–30% | | B and T cells: specific immunity |
| — monocytes | 2–8% | | become **macrophages** |
| — eosinophils | 1–4% | | allergy, worms |
| — basophils | < 1% | | **histamine**, **heparin** |
| **Platelets** (thrombocytes) | 1.5–4 lakh/µL | 7–10 days | clotting |

- **Erythropoiesis** happens in red bone marrow, stimulated by **erythropoietin** from the kidney.
- **Clotting** needs **Ca²⁺** and **vitamin K** (for factors II, VII, IX, X): prothrombin → thrombin; fibrinogen → fibrin. Haemophilia = inherited lack of factor VIII (A) or IX (B).
- **Blood groups**: **ABO** (Karl Landsteiner, 1900–01) and **Rh** (Landsteiner and Wiener, 1940). **O negative** = universal donor; **AB positive** = universal recipient. Rh incompatibility → erythroblastosis fetalis.

## 5. The immune system

**Antigen** — any substance the body sees as foreign that triggers the production of **antibodies**; the exact spot an antibody binds is the **epitope**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q34]]

**Lymphoid organs:**
- **Primary** (where lymphocytes mature): **red bone marrow** (B cells) and **thymus** (T cells; hormone **thymosin**).
- **Secondary** (where they meet antigens): **lymph nodes**, **spleen** (largest lymphoid organ), **tonsils**, **Peyer's patches** and other mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT), appendix.

So the **adrenal and thyroid glands are not lymphoid** tissues; tonsils and spleen are. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q50]]

**Innate vs adaptive immunity:**

| | **Innate** (natural, non-specific) | **Adaptive** (acquired, specific) |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | present from birth, **genetically determined** | develops after exposure to a specific antigen |
| Speed | immediate | days (faster on re-exposure) |
| Memory | none | **memory cells** |
| Parts | barriers (skin, mucus, tears with **lysozyme**, stomach acid), phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages), **NK cells**, complement, interferons, **inflammation** (redness, heat, swelling, pain: regional inflammation and local defence in tissues), fever | **B cells** (humoral) and **T cells** (cell-mediated) |

**Two arms of adaptive immunity:**
- **B-cell (humoral) immunity** — B cells become **plasma cells** that produce **antibodies**.
- **T-cell (cell-mediated) immunity** — T cells act directly in the tissues: **helper T (CD4)** coordinate (the cells HIV destroys); **cytotoxic T (CD8)** kill infected and cancer cells; regulatory T restrain the response.

Match: adaptive immunity → immunity to a specific antigen on exposure; T-cell immunity → action in the tissues; innate immunity → genetically determined; B-cell immunity → production of antibodies. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q133]]

**Antibodies (immunoglobulins)** [[LIKELY]] — Y-shaped: 2 heavy + 2 light chains. Five classes:
- **IgG**: ~75–80%, the only one that **crosses the placenta**;
- **IgA**: in secretions (milk, colostrum, saliva, tears);
- **IgM**: a pentamer, the largest, **first** to appear in an infection;
- **IgE**: allergy and parasites (binds mast cells);
- **IgD**: receptor on B cells.

**Types of acquired immunity:** **active** — natural (after infection) or artificial (**vaccination**; Edward Jenner, smallpox, 1796); **passive** — natural (antibodies across the placenta and in breast milk) or artificial (ready-made antibodies: antitoxin, antiserum).

**Disorders:** hypersensitivity (allergy, anaphylaxis), autoimmunity (rheumatoid arthritis, type 1 diabetes, Graves' disease), immunodeficiency (HIV/AIDS).

**Mind and immunity** — **psychoneuroimmunology** (Robert Ader and Nicholas Cohen, 1975): chronic stress and cortisol suppress immunity; yoga and meditation studies report better immune markers.

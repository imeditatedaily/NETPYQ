# Personality

> **At a glance** — Latin *persona* = mask · Allport's definition (1937) · types: **Hippocrates** (4 humours), **Kretschmer** (pyknic, athletic, asthenic), **Sheldon** (endo-, meso-, ectomorph), **Jung** (introvert, extravert), **Friedman & Rosenman** (Type A, Type B) · traits: **Allport**, **Cattell** (16 PF), **Eysenck**, **Big Five** · psychoanalytic: **Freud** (id, ego, superego; unconscious; 5 psychosexual stages) · **Adler** = individual psychology · **Murray**: "the history of the personality is the personality" · humanistic: **Maslow** (hierarchy of needs), **Rogers** · Indian: **triguṇa**, **16 mānasa prakṛti**

Personality is the most-asked psychology topic in Unit 6 (23 questions so far). Know each theorist's name, terms and order of stages.

## 1. Nature and characteristics of personality

**Word:** Latin *persona*, the mask worn by actors in Roman theatre — the face we show to the world.

**Gordon Allport (1937):** personality is "the **dynamic organisation** within the individual of those **psychophysical systems** that determine his **unique adjustments** to his environment."

**Characteristics** — personality is:
- a **characteristic pattern of behaviour**, and of **modes of thinking**, feeling and acting; [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q65]]
- **unique** to each person, yet **consistent** across time and situations;
- **dynamic** (it develops and changes);
- **psychophysical** (both mind and body);
- organised and integrated; shaped by heredity and environment together.

**Determinants of personality** (व्यक्तित्व के निर्धारक):

| Determinant | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Biological** | heredity (genes, temperament), **physique** and appearance, **endocrine glands** (thyroid: overactive = restless, underactive = sluggish; adrenals; gonads), nervous system, health |
| **Psychological** | intelligence, motives and needs, emotions, self-concept, attitudes |
| **Family** | parenting style, birth order (Adler), siblings, home atmosphere |
| **Social** | school, teachers, peers, mass media, social roles |
| **Cultural** | values, customs, religion, language |
| **Situational** | life events, crises |

[[LIKELY]] Endocrine determinants (thyroid, adrenal) and the heredity–environment debate.

## 2. Type theories

Type theories put people into **categories**.

| Theorist | Types | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Hippocrates** (Greek; later Galen) | **sanguine** (blood: cheerful), **choleric** (yellow bile: irritable), **melancholic** (black bile: sad), **phlegmatic** (phlegm: calm, slow) | four humours |
| **Ernst Kretschmer** (1921, *Physique and Character*) | **pyknic** (short, round, stocky: sociable, mood swings; prone to manic-depression), **athletic** (muscular: energetic), **asthenic / leptosomic** (tall, thin: reserved, prone to schizophrenia), dysplastic (mixed) | body build |
| **William Sheldon** (1940s) | **endomorph** (soft, round → *viscerotonia*: relaxed, loves comfort and food), **mesomorph** (muscular → *somatotonia*: assertive, active), **ectomorph** (thin, fragile → *cerebrotonia*: restrained, intellectual) | somatotype |
| **Carl Jung** (1921, *Psychological Types*) | **introvert** (energy turned inward) and **extravert** (outward); later others added the **ambivert** | attitude of the psyche |
| **Friedman & Rosenman** (1959), cardiologists studying traits and **coronary heart disease** | **Type A** (competitive, impatient, time-urgent, hostile → higher CHD risk) and **Type B** (relaxed, easy-going) | behaviour pattern |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P2-Q14, YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q074]]

**Matching triad that has been asked:** **Ascendant → Allport** (ascendance–submission scale), **Athletic → Kretschmer**, **Introvert → Jung**, **Ectomorphic → Sheldon**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q88]]

[[LIKELY]] Type C (cancer-prone: suppressed emotion) and Type D (Denollet: "distressed" — negative affect with social inhibition); Sheldon's temperament names.

## 3. Trait theories

Trait theories describe personality by **dimensions** everyone has to some degree.

- **Gordon Allport** — traits: **cardinal** (one dominant trait shapes the whole life), **central** (5–10 main traits), **secondary** (situational preferences). With F. H. Allport he built the **ascendance–submission** reaction study (1928).
- **Raymond Cattell** — used **factor analysis**; **surface traits** vs **source traits**; **16 PF** (Sixteen Personality Factor) questionnaire.
- **Hans Eysenck** — three super-factors: **extraversion–introversion**, **neuroticism–stability**, **psychoticism** (PEN model); EPQ.
- **Big Five** (McCrae & Costa; "OCEAN"): **O**penness, **C**onscientiousness, **E**xtraversion, **A**greeableness, **N**euroticism. [[LIKELY]]

**Descriptive vs developmental theories** — type, trait and factor-analytic theories *describe* the structure of personality; **psychoanalytic** and **learning** theories explain how personality **develops** over time, so they are the developmental theories. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q43]]

## 4. Psychoanalytic theory: Freud

**Sigmund Freud** (1856–1939) is the father of **psychoanalysis** and of the psychoanalytic theory of personality. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q63]]

**Levels of mind (topographic model):** conscious, preconscious, **unconscious**. The unconscious is the **deepest and largest** part, the submerged bulk of the iceberg, holding repressed wishes, instincts and memories. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q072]]

**Structure of personality (structural model):** the three components are **id, ego and superego** ("self" is not one of them). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P2-Q23]]

| | **Id** | **Ego** | **Superego** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | **biological**, inborn, instincts (libido, *eros* and *thanatos*) | psychological; "**executive**" | social; "**ethical / moral** branch" |
| Principle | **pleasure principle** (immediate gratification) | **reality principle** | **morality principle** (perfection) |
| Level | **wholly unconscious** | conscious, preconscious and unconscious | mostly unconscious |
| Parts | — | mediates between id, superego and reality; uses **defence mechanisms** | **conscience** (guilt) + **ego ideal** (pride) |

The id is identified by the **pleasure principle** and by being **absolutely unconscious**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q115]]

**Dreams** — Freud called dreams "**the royal road to the unconscious**" (*The Interpretation of Dreams*, 1900); manifest content hides latent content through condensation, displacement and symbolism. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q130]]

**Psychosexual stages** (in order): [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q131]]

| Stage | Age | Erogenous zone | Key conflict / fixation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. **Oral** | 0–1 yr | mouth | weaning; oral fixation (smoking, overeating) |
| 2. **Anal** | 1–3 yr | anus | toilet training; anal-retentive / expulsive |
| 3. **Phallic** | 3–6 yr | genitals | **Oedipus complex** (boys), **Electra complex** (girls; term from Jung) |
| 4. **Latency** | 6–puberty | dormant | sexual energy channelled into school, friends |
| 5. **Genital** | puberty onward | genitals | mature sexuality |

**Defence mechanisms** (worked out further by Anna Freud, 1936) [[LIKELY]]: **repression** (pushing out of awareness: the basic one), **denial**, **projection** (attributing one's own impulses to others), **displacement** (onto a safer target), **rationalisation** (good-sounding excuses), **reaction formation** (acting opposite), **regression** (going back to childish behaviour), **sublimation** (channelling into socially valued activity: the only "healthy" one), intellectualisation.

## 5. The neo-Freudians and Murray

- **Alfred Adler** — founder of **individual psychology** (वैयक्तिक मनोविज्ञान). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q089]] Key ideas: **inferiority complex**, compensation, **striving for superiority**, **style of life**, **birth order**, social interest.
- **Carl Jung** — **analytical psychology**. The psyche: ego, **personal unconscious** (complexes), **collective unconscious** (inherited) with **archetypes**: persona, shadow, anima/animus, self. Process of **individuation**. Attitudes: introversion–extraversion. **Four psychological functions**: **thinking and feeling** (rational) and **sensation and intuition** (irrational) — in Hindi, *cintana–bhāvanā* and *saṃvedanā–sahaja bodha*. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q36]] Stages of life: **childhood**, **youth / young adulthood**, **middle age** (crisis and turning inward, after ~40), old age. [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q090]]
- **Karen Horney** (basic anxiety; moving towards, against, away from people), **Erich Fromm**, **Harry Stack Sullivan** (interpersonal theory).
- **Erik Erikson** — **eight psychosocial stages** [[LIKELY]]: trust vs mistrust (infancy) → autonomy vs shame → initiative vs guilt → industry vs inferiority → **identity vs role confusion** (adolescence) → intimacy vs isolation → generativity vs stagnation → integrity vs despair (old age).
- **Henry Murray** — **personology**: "**The history of the personality is the personality**." Needs (*n*-achievement, *n*-affiliation, …) and environmental **press**; **Thematic Apperception Test (TAT)** with Christiana Morgan (1935). [[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q083]]

## 6. Humanistic theories: Maslow and Rogers

The humanistic approach to personality ("third force" after psychoanalysis and behaviourism) was developed by **Carl Rogers** and **Abraham Maslow**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q55]] It stresses free will, the whole person and growth.

**Abraham Maslow** proposed the **hierarchical model** — the **hierarchy of needs** (1943; *Motivation and Personality*, 1954). [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q87]] From the base upward:

1. **Physiological / biological** needs (food, water, sleep)
2. **Safety** needs (security, shelter)
3. **Belongingness and love** (affiliation and affection)
4. **Esteem** (self-respect, recognition)
5. **Self-actualisation** (self-attainment: becoming all one can be)

[[PYQ: YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q81, YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q088]]

The lower four are **deficiency (D) needs**, self-actualisation a **growth (B) need**. Maslow also wrote of **peak experiences** and, later, **self-transcendence** above self-actualisation. [[LIKELY]]

**Carl Rogers** — person-centred theory: **self-concept**; **real self vs ideal self**; **congruence** vs incongruence; **unconditional positive regard**; the **fully functioning person**; **client-centred therapy**.

**Social-cognitive theories** [[LIKELY]]: Bandura (self-efficacy, reciprocal determinism), Julian Rotter (**locus of control**: internal vs external), Walter Mischel.

## 7. Assessment of personality

| Method | Tools |
|---|---|
| **Self-report inventories** | MMPI (Hathaway & McKinley, 1943), 16 PF (Cattell), EPQ (Eysenck), NEO-PI (Big Five) |
| **Projective tests** | **Rorschach inkblot test** (1921, 10 cards), **TAT** (Murray & Morgan), sentence completion, draw-a-person |
| **Behavioural assessment** | **observation**, interview, rating scales, situational tests |

**Observation** is a popular method of assessing personality, because it studies an individual's behaviour pattern in **actual life situations**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q45]]

## 8. Indian theories: triguṇa and mānasa prakṛti

**Triguṇa** (Sāṃkhya; Gītā chapters 14, 17, 18) — every person is a mix of the three guṇas, and the predominant guṇa gives the personality type:

| Guṇa | Marks (Gītā 14) | Personality |
|---|---|---|
| **Sattva** | light (*prakāśa*), knowledge, happiness | **positive attitude, happiness, lightness, spiritual awakening and consciousness** [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q17]]; calm, detached, truthful |
| **Rajas** | passion (*rāga*), craving, attachment, restless activity; *lobha* (greed) arises from rajas (14.17) | active, ambitious, restless |
| **Tamas** | darkness (*aprakāśa*), inertia, negligence (*pramāda*), delusion (*moha*), sleep | dull, lazy, **lacking enlightenment**; in Caraka's tāmasa types, **greedy** especially for food |

The key for the tāmasika personality chose **greedy** and **lack of enlightenment**; note that the Gītā itself assigns greed to rajas (14.17), while Caraka's tāmasa types are described as gluttonous and dull. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q42]]

**Mānasa prakṛti** (psychological constitution; Caraka Śārīra 4.36–40, Suśruta Śārīra 4) — **16** types in all: [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q17]]
- **7 sāttvika**: brāhma, ārṣa, aindra (māhendra), yāmya, vāruṇa, kaubera, gāndharva;
- **6 rājasa**: āsura, rākṣasa, paiśāca, sārpa, praita, śākuna;
- **3 tāmasa**: pāśava, mātsya, vānaspatya.

**Deha prakṛti** (bodily constitution): 7 types from the doṣas — vāta, pitta, kapha, three dual types, and *sama* (balanced).

**Pañcakośa** (Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2): annamaya, prāṇamaya, manomaya, vijñānamaya, ānandamaya — a layered model of the person used in yoga psychology.

[[LIKELY]] Matching sāttvika/rājasa/tāmasa types with qualities; number of sāttvika types (7); pañcakośa order.

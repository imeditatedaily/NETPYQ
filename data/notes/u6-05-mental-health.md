# Mental Health: Stress, Frustration, Conflict and Disorders

> **At a glance** — WHO health definition (1948) includes **mental** well-being · sources of frustration: **environmental, personal, conflict** · Lewin's conflicts: approach–approach, avoidance–avoidance, approach–avoidance (+ double approach–avoidance) · **Hans Selye**: "stress is the non-specific response of the body to any demand"; GAS = alarm → resistance → exhaustion · phobia = **anxiety disorder** · paranoid schizophrenia → antipsychotics such as **chlorpromazine** · **schizoid** PD = detached from social relationships · hallucination = perception without stimulus; illusion = false perception of a stimulus; delusion = false belief · psychodynamic therapy = **Freud's psychoanalysis + ego analysis**

The syllabus line: "Mental health; conflicts and frustration; insomnia, depression, stress, anxiety." (Insomnia is covered in *Consciousness, Altered States and Sleep*.)

## 1. Mental health and abnormality

**WHO (1948):** "Health is a state of complete **physical, mental and social well-being** and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." WHO's current description of **mental health**: a state of mental well-being that enables people to **cope with the stresses of life**, realise their abilities, learn well and work well, and contribute to their community.

**Marks of a mentally healthy person** (Marie Jahoda, 1958, *Current Concepts of Positive Mental Health*): positive attitude towards oneself; growth and self-actualisation; integration of personality; autonomy; **accurate perception of reality**; environmental mastery. Others add: insight into oneself, rational behaviour, good social relationships, emotional maturity.

**Behaviour that does not resemble a normal, healthy person's**: **social maladjustment** and **bizarre behaviour**. Insightful behaviour, knowledge of reality and rational behaviour are signs of health. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q104]]

**Criteria for abnormality** — the "four Ds": **deviance** (statistically rare or against social norms), **distress**, **dysfunction** (interferes with daily life), **danger** (to self or others).

**Research methods of abnormal psychology:** **clinical case study**, correlational and epidemiological studies, the experiment, **analogue experiments** (producing a disorder-like state in the laboratory or in animals), **single-case experimental designs** (e.g. ABAB reversal), longitudinal studies. A "hypothetical method" is not one of them. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q071]]

## 2. Frustration and conflict

**Frustration** (कुण्ठा) — the feeling when a goal-directed behaviour is **blocked**.

**Major sources of frustration:**
1. **Environmental factors** — physical obstacles (floods, traffic) and social ones (rules, prejudice, poverty);
2. **Personal factors** — one's own limitations (lack of ability, physical handicap, unrealistic aims);
3. **Conflict** — two motives that cannot both be satisfied.

Positive goals themselves are not a source. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q33]]

**Conflict between two opposing motives is a major source of frustration**, because when one motive is satisfied the other is frustrated. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q69]]

**Types of conflict** (Kurt Lewin, 1935; Neal Miller):

| Type | Situation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Approach–approach** | two attractive goals | two good job offers (easiest to resolve) |
| **Avoidance–avoidance** | two unpleasant choices | study for an exam you hate, or fail ("between the devil and the deep sea") |
| **Approach–avoidance** | one goal with good and bad sides | a well-paid job far from home (most stressful; vacillation) |
| **Double (multiple) approach–avoidance** | two goals, each with good and bad sides | choosing between two colleges, each with pros and cons |

**Reactions to frustration:** aggression (**frustration–aggression hypothesis**, Dollard and colleagues, 1939), withdrawal, regression, apathy, fantasy, defence mechanisms, or constructive problem solving.

## 3. Stress

**Hans Selye** (Canadian endocrinologist; "father of stress research"): "**Stress is the non-specific response of the body to any demand made upon it.**" [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S68]]

**General adaptation syndrome (GAS)** (Selye, 1936):
1. **Alarm reaction** — shock, then counter-shock: sympathetic arousal, adrenaline, cortisol;
2. **Resistance** — the body adapts, stays aroused; resources are spent;
3. **Exhaustion** — resources run out: illness ("diseases of adaptation": hypertension, ulcers, lowered immunity).

**Eustress** (good stress that motivates) vs **distress** (harmful) — also Selye's terms.

**Other stress models** [[LIKELY]]:
- **Walter Cannon** — the "**fight-or-flight**" response (1915) and **homeostasis** (*The Wisdom of the Body*, 1932).
- **Richard Lazarus & Susan Folkman** (1984) — **transactional model**: stress depends on **primary appraisal** (is this a threat?) and **secondary appraisal** (can I cope?). **Coping**: **problem-focused** vs **emotion-focused**.
- **Holmes and Rahe** (1967) — **Social Readjustment Rating Scale**: life events in life-change units; death of a spouse = 100.
- **Yerkes–Dodson law** (1908) — performance rises with arousal up to an optimum, then falls (inverted U).
- Daily hassles (Kanner, Lazarus); burnout; learned helplessness (Martin **Seligman**, 1967).

**Physiology of stress** (two pathways; details in *Endocrine System*):
- **SAM axis** (sympathetic–adrenal–medullary): fast; sympathetic nerves and **adrenaline/noradrenaline** from the adrenal medulla.
- **HPA axis** (hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal): slower; **CRH → ACTH → cortisol**.
- Long-term cortisol raises blood sugar and blood pressure, weakens immunity, and damages the hippocampus (memory).

**Yoga and stress** — yoga shifts autonomic balance towards the parasympathetic, lowers cortisol and raises heart-rate variability. In the Yoga Sūtra, the obstacles (*antarāya*, 1.30) come with sorrow, dejection, trembling of the body and irregular breathing (*duḥkha, daurmanasya, aṅgam-ejayatva, śvāsa-praśvāsa*, 1.31) — a classical description of the stress state.

## 4. Anxiety and depression

**Anxiety disorders** (DSM-5-TR):

| Disorder | Core feature |
|---|---|
| **Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD)** | excessive, uncontrollable **worry** about many things, most days for **≥ 6 months**; restlessness, fatigue, muscle tension, poor sleep |
| **Panic disorder** | recurrent unexpected **panic attacks** (palpitations, breathlessness, fear of dying) |
| **Specific phobia** | intense, irrational fear of a specific object or situation, with avoidance: acrophobia (heights), claustrophobia (closed spaces), hydrophobia (water) |
| **Agoraphobia** | fear of places where escape is hard (crowds, open spaces) |
| **Social anxiety disorder** (social phobia) | fear of being judged in social situations |
| Separation anxiety, selective mutism | mostly in children |

A **phobia is an anxiety disorder**; the key's option was worded "generalised anxiety disorder", the only option naming the anxiety family. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q33]] (Obsessive-compulsive disorder and PTSD now have their own DSM chapters.)

**Depression** — **major depressive disorder** (**unipolar**): for **≥ 2 weeks**, at least 5 of 9 symptoms, including **depressed mood** or **anhedonia** (loss of pleasure); others are weight/appetite change, sleep change (insomnia or hypersomnia), psychomotor change, fatigue, worthlessness or guilt, poor concentration, thoughts of death. **Persistent depressive disorder** (dysthymia): milder, **≥ 2 years**. **Bipolar disorder**: episodes of **mania** and depression. Biological factors: low **serotonin** and noradrenaline (monoamine hypothesis), HPA overactivity.

## 5. Psychotic disorders, personality disorders and symptoms

**Schizophrenia** (term by Eugen **Bleuler**, 1908–11; Emil Kraepelin's "dementia praecox"):
- **Positive symptoms**: **delusions**, **hallucinations** (usually auditory), disorganised speech and behaviour.
- **Negative symptoms**: flat affect, avolition, alogia, social withdrawal.
- **Paranoid** type (delusions of persecution or grandeur; subtypes were dropped in DSM-5, 2013).
- **Treatment**: **antipsychotic drugs**. **Chlorpromazine** (1952, Delay and Deniker) was the first; it blocks **dopamine D2** receptors (dopamine hypothesis). Later: haloperidol; atypicals such as clozapine, risperidone. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q073]]

**Personality disorders** (DSM clusters):
- **Cluster A** (odd, eccentric): paranoid, **schizoid** (detachment from social relationships, little interest in them, restricted emotion — lacking social skills), schizotypal.
- **Cluster B** (dramatic, emotional): antisocial, borderline, histrionic, narcissistic.
- **Cluster C** (anxious, fearful): avoidant, dependent, obsessive-compulsive.

A person who cannot maintain social relationships and has no interest in them shows **schizoid personality disorder**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q071]]

**Symptoms to tell apart:**

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Hallucination** | **perception without a stimulus** (hearing voices that are not there) |
| **Illusion** | **false perception of a real stimulus** (a rope seen as a snake — the Vedāntic example) |
| **Delusion** | **fixed false belief** not shared by one's culture |
| **Depressive disorder** | **unipolar** mood disorder |

## 6. Therapies

| Approach | Therapies | Key figures |
|---|---|---|
| **Psychodynamic** | **Freud's psychoanalytic therapy** (free association, dream analysis, transference, resistance, interpretation); **ego analysis** / ego psychology (Anna Freud, Heinz Hartmann); brief psychodynamic therapy | Freud |
| **Behaviour therapy** | **systematic desensitisation** (Joseph Wolpe, 1958) for phobias, aversion therapy, flooding, token economy, modelling | Wolpe, Skinner, Bandura |
| **Cognitive** | **cognitive therapy** (Aaron Beck: negative automatic thoughts, cognitive triad in depression); **REBT** (Albert Ellis, ABC model); CBT | Beck, Ellis |
| **Humanistic–existential** | **client-centred therapy** (Rogers), **Gestalt therapy** (Fritz Perls), logotherapy (Viktor Frankl) | |
| **Multimodal** | BASIC ID (Arnold Lazarus) | |
| **Biomedical** | drugs (antidepressants, anxiolytics, antipsychotics), ECT | |

So the parts of **psychodynamic** therapy are **Freud's psychoanalytic therapy** and **ego analysis**; cognitive, Gestalt and multimodal therapy belong to other schools. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q105]]

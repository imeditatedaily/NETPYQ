# Consciousness, Altered States and Sleep

> **At a glance** — consciousness has **two functions** (monitoring, controlling) · ASC defined by **Arnold Ludwig (1966)** · EEG discovered by **Hans Berger (1929)** · **REM sleep discovered by Aserinsky and Kleitman (1953)** · NREM: **four stages** (Rechtschaffen and Kales, 1968) → **three** (N1, N2, N3; AASM, **2007**) · one cycle ≈ **90 minutes**, 4–6 cycles a night · master clock: **suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus** · study of biological rhythms: **chronobiology**

The syllabus line is "Introduction to altered states of consciousness; sleep: stages of sleep; sleep disorders". Questions so far have asked the number of NREM stages twice, where the circadian clock sits, what chronobiology is, and the functions of consciousness.

## 1. Consciousness

**Consciousness** (चेतना) is our awareness of ourselves and our environment: of sensations, thoughts, feelings and memories at a given moment.

- **William James** (1890): consciousness is a continuous, changing **"stream of consciousness"**, not a chain of separate pieces.
- **Two functions** of consciousness: **monitoring** (keeping track of ourselves and our surroundings) and **controlling** (planning, starting and guiding our actions). [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q082]]
- **Freud's levels**: **conscious** (what we are aware of now), **preconscious** (can be recalled at will), **unconscious** (largest; repressed wishes and memories): the "iceberg".
- **Levels of awareness** in modern psychology: controlled processes (full attention, effortful) → automatic processes (little attention: typing, driving a known route) → daydreaming → sleep and dreams → unconscious processes.
- **Flow of awareness of a sensation in the body**: stimulation of **receptors** → **sensory nerve impulses** along the sensory pathway (through the spinal cord) → **sensory area of the cerebral cortex** → **translation into sensation and perception** → **awareness and attention** to that particular sensation. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q132]]

**Yoga's map of consciousness** (for linking questions): Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad's four states — **jāgrat** (waking, *vaiśvānara*), **svapna** (dream, *taijasa*), **suṣupti** (deep sleep, *prājña*), and **turīya** (the fourth, pure consciousness). Yoga Sūtra 1.10 defines sleep as a *vṛtti*: "the mental modification resting on the absence of content" (*abhāva-pratyaya-ālambanā vṛttir nidrā*).

## 2. Altered states of consciousness (ASC)

An **altered state of consciousness** (परिवर्तित चेतना अवस्था) is any mental state clearly different from normal waking consciousness in perception, thinking, emotion, sense of time or sense of self. **Arnold Ludwig (1966)** gave the classic definition; **Charles Tart** (1969, *Altered States of Consciousness*) made it a field of research.

**How ASCs are produced:**

| Route | Examples |
|---|---|
| Natural | **sleep and dreams**, daydreaming, hypnagogic states (falling asleep) |
| Reduced stimulation | **sensory deprivation**, isolation tanks, long monotony |
| Increased stimulation | rhythmic drumming, chanting, whirling dance |
| Psychological | **hypnosis**, **meditation**, prayer, trance, **yoga nidrā** |
| Physiological | fasting, high fever, **hyperventilation**, oxygen lack |
| Drugs (psychoactive) | depressants (alcohol), stimulants (caffeine, amphetamine), opiates, hallucinogens (LSD, mescaline, cannabis) |

**Common features (Ludwig):** changes in thinking, disturbed sense of time, loss of control, change in emotional expression, change in body image, perceptual distortions, change in meaning, sense of the ineffable, feelings of rejuvenation, hypersuggestibility.

**Hypnosis** — a state of focused attention and heightened **suggestibility**. Franz **Mesmer** ("animal magnetism", 18th century) → James **Braid** coined *hypnosis* (Greek *hypnos*, sleep; 1843). Ernest **Hilgard**: **neodissociation theory** and the "hidden observer". Hypnosis is *not* sleep: the EEG stays that of waking.

**Meditation** — the research landmarks:
- **Anand, Chhina and Singh (1961)**: rāja yogis in samādhi showed persistent **alpha** waves that outside stimuli did not block.
- **Kasamatsu and Hirai (1966)**: Zen monks — alpha, then slower alpha, then **theta** in long-term meditators.
- **R. K. Wallace (1970)**: Transcendental Meditation as a "**wakeful hypometabolic** state" (less oxygen use, lower heart rate, more skin resistance).
- **Herbert Benson (1975)**: the **relaxation response**, the opposite of fight-or-flight.

**EEG rhythms** (Hans Berger recorded the first human EEG and named the **alpha** wave, 1929):

| Wave | Frequency | State |
|---|---|---|
| Gamma | > 30 Hz | intense focus, binding of perception |
| **Beta** | 13–30 Hz | alert, active thinking, eyes open |
| **Alpha** | 8–13 Hz | **relaxed wakefulness with eyes closed**; relaxation and meditation |
| **Theta** | 4–8 Hz | drowsiness, **N1 sleep**, deep meditation, children |
| **Delta** | 0.5–4 Hz | **deep slow-wave sleep (N3)**, infants |

## 3. The discovery of sleep stages

| Year | Discovery | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1929 | First human EEG; alpha disappears in sleep | Hans Berger |
| 1937 | Sleep EEG divided into stages A–E | Loomis, Harvey and Hobart |
| **1953** | **Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep** discovered in sleeping subjects at the University of Chicago; REM linked with dreaming | **Eugene Aserinsky and Nathaniel Kleitman** |
| 1957 | The ~90-minute NREM–REM **sleep cycle** | William Dement and Kleitman |
| 1959 | Muscle atonia in REM; called "**paradoxical sleep**" | Michel Jouvet |
| **1968** | Standard scoring manual: NREM stages **1, 2, 3, 4** + REM | **Rechtschaffen and Kales** |
| **2007** | AASM manual: stages 3 and 4 merged into **N3**; stages now **N1, N2, N3, R** | **American Academy of Sleep Medicine** |

**How many NREM stages?** — **four** in the classical Rechtschaffen–Kales scheme, which older papers and textbooks use [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q22]]; **three** in the current AASM scheme. In June 2025 NTA accepted both "three" and "four". [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q132]] For a new question: answer *four* if it speaks of the classic stages 1–4, *three* if it speaks of N1–N3 or current standards.

## 4. Stages of sleep

Sleep has two kinds: **NREM** (non-rapid eye movement, about 75–80% of the night) and **REM** (about 20–25%).

| Stage | Share of night (adult) | EEG | Body and experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N1** (old stage 1) | ~5% | alpha gives way to **theta**; vertex sharp waves | drowsy, easily woken; **hypnic jerks**; slow rolling eye movements |
| **N2** (old stage 2) | ~45–55% (the largest) | **sleep spindles** (12–14 Hz bursts) and **K-complexes** | true sleep; heart rate and temperature fall |
| **N3** (old stages 3 + 4) | ~15–20% | **delta** (slow-wave sleep, SWS) ≥ 20% of the epoch | deepest, hardest to wake; **growth hormone** released; sleepwalking, night terrors, bed-wetting happen here |
| **REM** (R) | ~20–25% | low-voltage, fast, **waking-like** (beta/theta, "sawtooth" waves) | **rapid eye movements**, vivid **dreams**, **atonia** of skeletal muscles (except eyes and diaphragm), irregular breathing and heart rate, penile/clitoral erection: hence "**paradoxical sleep**" |

**Architecture of a night** [[LIKELY]]:
- Order in a cycle: N1 → N2 → N3 → N2 → **REM**; one cycle ≈ **90 minutes**; **4–6 cycles** a night.
- **N3 dominates the first third** of the night; **REM periods lengthen towards morning** (the last can last 30–60 min).
- Newborns sleep ~16–17 h with ~**50% REM**; REM share falls with age; deep sleep falls sharply in old age.
- Adults need **7–9 hours** (National Sleep Foundation).

**Why we sleep** (theories): **restorative** (repair, growth hormone, protein synthesis), **energy conservation**, **evolutionary/adaptive** (safety at night), **memory consolidation** (SWS for declarative, REM for procedural and emotional memory), **glymphatic clearance** of brain waste during sleep (Nedergaard, 2013). **Two-process model** (Borbély, 1982): **Process S** (sleep pressure, building with **adenosine** while awake) and **Process C** (circadian timing).

## 5. Circadian rhythm and chronobiology

- **Circadian** (Latin *circa diem*, "about a day"; term coined by **Franz Halberg**, 1959) rhythms repeat roughly every 24 hours: sleep–wake, body temperature (lowest ~4–5 am), cortisol (peaks after waking), melatonin (night).
- The **study of biological rhythms, including circadian rhythms, is chronobiology** (काल-जैविकी). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q15]]
- The **master "circadian clock"** is the **suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) of the hypothalamus**, just above the optic chiasma. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q27]]
- Light reaches the SCN from special retinal ganglion cells containing **melanopsin**, via the **retinohypothalamic tract**. The SCN signals the **pineal gland**, which secretes **melatonin** in darkness ("hormone of darkness").
- Molecular clock genes (*period*, *timeless*, *clock*): **Nobel Prize 2017** to Jeffrey Hall, Michael Rosbash and Michael Young.
- Other rhythms: **ultradian** (shorter than a day: the 90-min sleep cycle, the nasal cycle of *svara yoga*), **infradian** (longer: the menstrual cycle), **circannual** (seasonal).
- Yoga and Āyurveda link: *dinacaryā* (daily routine), *brāhma muhūrta* waking, and *ṛtucaryā* (seasonal routine) are rhythm-based regimens.

## 6. Sleep disorders

The **International Classification of Sleep Disorders (ICSD-3, 2014)** groups them:

| Group | Disorders | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Insomnia** (अनिद्रा) | chronic insomnia | difficulty falling or staying asleep or early waking, with daytime impairment; **chronic** = ≥ 3 nights a week for ≥ 3 months; the **most common** sleep disorder; linked to anxiety, depression, stress |
| Sleep-related breathing | **obstructive sleep apnoea** | airway collapses in sleep; snoring, choking, daytime sleepiness; obesity a major risk |
| **Hypersomnolence** | **narcolepsy** | sudden sleep attacks, **cataplexy** (sudden loss of muscle tone with emotion), sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations, sleep-onset REM; loss of **orexin (hypocretin)** neurons |
| Circadian rhythm sleep–wake | jet lag, shift-work disorder, delayed sleep phase | clock out of step with the day |
| **Parasomnias** — NREM | **sleepwalking (somnambulism)**, **night terrors**, confusional arousals | from **N3**, first third of the night; little memory afterwards |
| Parasomnias — REM | **nightmares**, **REM sleep behaviour disorder** (acting out dreams: atonia fails), sleep paralysis | later in the night |
| Movement | **restless legs syndrome**, periodic limb movements, **bruxism** (teeth grinding) | |
| Others | **enuresis** (bed-wetting) | |

**Yoga for sleep:** the Gītā asks for moderation in sleep as in food (*na cāti-svapna-śīlasya jāgrato naiva cārjuna*, 6.16; *yukta-svapnāvabodhasya*, 6.17). Practices with research support for insomnia: yoga nidrā, śavāsana, bhrāmarī, slow breathing with long exhalation, and regular *dinacaryā*.

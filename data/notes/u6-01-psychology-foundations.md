# Psychology: Nature, Schools, Methods and Behaviourism

> **At a glance** — Psychology (मनोविज्ञान) = the scientific study of **behaviour and mental processes** · first laboratory: **Wilhelm Wundt, Leipzig, 1879** · behaviourism: **J. B. Watson, 1913** · S-O-R model: **R. S. Woodworth** · APA founded **1892**, APS (American Psychological Society) **1988** · learning: **Thorndike** (trial and error), **Pavlov** (classical conditioning), **Skinner** (operant conditioning), **Köhler** (insight)

The syllabus asks for "behavioural psychology" and the foundations needed for everything else in Unit 6. This chapter builds that base: what psychology is, how it grew from philosophy into a science, its schools, its methods, and the learning theories that behaviourism produced.

## 1. What psychology is

**Word:** Greek *psyche* (soul, breath of life) + *logos* (study). The meaning of the subject changed as the science grew:

| Period | Psychology was defined as the study of | Why it changed |
|---|---|---|
| Greek philosophy (Plato, Aristotle) | the **soul** | the soul cannot be observed |
| 16th–17th century (Descartes) | the **mind** | "mind" was too vague to measure |
| 19th century (Wundt, James) | **consciousness** | consciousness can only be reported, not seen |
| early 20th century (Watson) | **behaviour** | observable and measurable |
| today | **behaviour and mental processes** | cognition came back as a measurable science |

**Modern definition** — psychology is the **scientific study of behaviour and mental processes** (मानव व्यवहार और मानसिक प्रक्रियाओं का वैज्ञानिक अध्ययन). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q10]] "Behaviour" means anything an organism does that can be observed (walking, speaking, a change in heart rate). "Mental processes" are the private experiences we infer from behaviour: sensation, perception, attention, memory, thinking, emotion, motivation.

**Goals of psychology:** to **describe**, **explain**, **predict** and **control** (or change) behaviour.

**What the mind can do** — the cognitive attributes of mind are **imagination, inference and deduction** (with perception, memory, reasoning, judgement). Hypnosis is a *state* that can be induced in the mind, and telepathy is not an accepted attribute at all. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q81]]

**Branches** (the ones questions name): general, experimental, physiological/biological, developmental (child, adolescent, ageing), social, educational, clinical (diagnosis and therapy of disorders), counselling, abnormal (study of disordered behaviour), industrial–organisational, health psychology, sports psychology, environmental, cross-cultural, positive psychology, and **yoga psychology** (the Indian psychology of the Yoga Sūtra and the Upaniṣads).

## 2. Milestones and schools of psychology

| Year | Event | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 1860 | *Elements of Psychophysics*: measuring sensation | Gustav Fechner |
| **1879** | **First psychology laboratory, Leipzig**: psychology becomes a separate science | **Wilhelm Wundt** ("father of experimental psychology") |
| 1885 | First experiments on memory (nonsense syllables) | Hermann Ebbinghaus |
| 1890 | *The Principles of Psychology* | William James ("father of American psychology") |
| **1892** | **American Psychological Association (APA)**; first president G. Stanley Hall | — |
| 1900 | *The Interpretation of Dreams*: psychoanalysis | Sigmund Freud |
| 1905 | First intelligence test | Binet and Simon |
| 1912 | Phi phenomenon: Gestalt psychology begins | Max Wertheimer |
| **1913** | "Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It": behaviourism | **John B. Watson** |
| 1938 | *The Behavior of Organisms*: operant conditioning | B. F. Skinner |
| 1954–62 | Humanistic psychology, the "third force" | Abraham Maslow, Carl Rogers |
| 1967 | *Cognitive Psychology*: the cognitive revolution | Ulric Neisser |
| **1988** | **American Psychological Society (APS)** founded by academic and research psychologists who wanted a science-centred body; renamed *Association for Psychological Science* in 2006 | — |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q072]]

**The schools** (सम्प्रदाय):

| School | Founder(s) | Studies | Method | Key idea |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Structuralism** | Wundt; **E. B. Titchener** (named it) | structure of conscious experience | introspection | break experience into elements: sensations, images, feelings |
| **Functionalism** | **William James**, John Dewey, J. R. Angell | what consciousness is *for* | introspection + observation | "stream of consciousness"; mind helps us adapt (Darwin's influence) |
| **Behaviourism** | **J. B. Watson**, B. F. Skinner | observable behaviour only | experiment, conditioning | S–R: behaviour is learned through conditioning |
| **Gestalt** | **Max Wertheimer**, Wolfgang Köhler, Kurt Koffka | perception as organised wholes | experiment | "the whole is different from the sum of its parts" |
| **Psychoanalysis** | **Sigmund Freud** | the unconscious | case study, free association | behaviour driven by unconscious conflicts |
| **Humanistic** | **Maslow, Rogers** | the whole person, growth | case study, interview | free will, self-actualisation |
| **Cognitive** | Neisser, Miller, Broadbent | thinking, memory, attention | experiment, computer models | mind as an information-processing system |
| Biological / neuroscience | — | brain, hormones, genes | brain imaging, EEG | behaviour has a physical basis |

**S-O-R model** — Watson's behaviourism was an **S–R** (stimulus → response) psychology. **R. S. Woodworth** (1929) put the **organism** between the two: the same stimulus produces different responses because of what is inside the organism (its motives, past learning, physiological state). Stimulus → **Organism** → Response. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q071]] [[LIKELY]] Questions can pair schools with founders or methods (structuralism–Titchener–introspection; functionalism–James).

## 3. Methods of psychology

| Method | What it is | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Introspection** (अन्तर्दर्शन) | looking into one's own mind and reporting it (Wundt, Titchener) | direct access to experience | subjective, cannot be checked; impossible for children and animals |
| **Observation** (निरीक्षण) | watching behaviour as it happens: **naturalistic** (real life) or **controlled** (laboratory); participant or non-participant | studies behaviour in actual life situations | observer bias; the observed may change behaviour |
| **Experimental** (प्रयोगात्मक) | changing an **independent variable** and measuring its effect on a **dependent variable** while controlling the rest; experimental vs control group | the only method that shows **cause and effect** | artificial; ethical limits |
| **Case study** (व्यक्ति-अध्ययन) | deep study of one person or group | rich detail; rare conditions | cannot be generalised |
| **Clinical method** | diagnosis and treatment of individuals with problems, from history, interview and tests | applied value | depends on the clinician |
| **Survey / questionnaire** | many people answer the same questions | large samples, quick | people may not answer honestly |
| **Correlational** | measures how two variables go together (r from −1 to +1) | shows relationships, aids prediction | **correlation is not causation** |
| **Psychological tests** | standardised tools for intelligence, aptitude, personality | objective scoring, norms | culture bias |
| **Differential method** | compares individuals or groups (sex, age, culture) | studies individual differences | many uncontrolled factors |
| **Developmental**: longitudinal and cross-sectional | same people followed over years / different age groups compared at once | shows change with age | slow and costly / cohort effects |

**Abnormal behaviour** is studied by the **clinical / psychoanalytic** approach: Freud built psychoanalysis from studying and treating neurotic, that is abnormal, patients. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q14]] (The research methods of abnormal psychology are in the *Mental Health* chapter.)

## 4. Behavioural psychology and learning

**Learning** (अधिगम) is a relatively permanent change in behaviour that comes from **experience or practice** (not from maturation, fatigue or drugs). Behaviourism explained almost all behaviour as learned. Four classic theories:

| Theory | Psychologist | Experiment | Key idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trial and error** (प्रयास और त्रुटि), **connectionism** | **E. L. Thorndike** (1898) | a hungry **cat in a puzzle box** learns to pull a latch to reach fish | stimulus–response bonds; correct responses are "stamped in" |
| **Classical conditioning** (शास्त्रीय अनुबन्धन) | **Ivan Pavlov** | **dog salivating** to a bell paired with food | learning by association of two stimuli |
| **Operant / instrumental conditioning** (क्रियाप्रसूत अनुबन्धन) | **B. F. Skinner** | rat or pigeon in an **operant conditioning chamber ("Skinner box")** presses a lever for food | behaviour is shaped by its consequences |
| **Insight learning** (अन्तर्दृष्टि) | **Wolfgang Köhler** (Gestalt) | chimpanzee **Sultan** joins two **sticks**, or stacks **boxes**, to reach bananas (Tenerife, *The Mentality of Apes*, 1917) | sudden grasp of the whole problem ("aha!") |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q69, YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q122]]

**Thorndike's laws:** law of **effect** (responses followed by satisfaction are strengthened), law of **exercise** (use strengthens, disuse weakens), law of **readiness**.

**Pavlov's terms:** unconditioned stimulus (UCS: food) → unconditioned response (UCR: salivation); neutral stimulus (bell) paired with UCS becomes the **conditioned stimulus (CS)**, which alone produces the **conditioned response (CR)**. Also: acquisition, **extinction** (CS without UCS weakens the CR), **spontaneous recovery**, **stimulus generalisation**, **discrimination**. Watson's **"Little Albert"** (1920) conditioned fear of a white rat.

**Skinner's terms:**
- **Reinforcement** increases behaviour: **positive** (adding something pleasant, e.g. praise) and **negative** (removing something unpleasant, e.g. a seat-belt buzzer stops). Negative reinforcement is *not* punishment.
- **Punishment** decreases behaviour: positive (adding something unpleasant) and negative (removing something pleasant).
- **Schedules:** continuous; partial — fixed ratio, variable ratio (most resistant to extinction: gambling), fixed interval, variable interval.
- **Shaping:** reinforcing successive approximations.

**Later learning theories** [[LIKELY]]:
- **Observational / social learning** — **Albert Bandura**, **Bobo doll** experiment (1961): children imitate an aggressive model. Steps: attention, retention, reproduction, motivation. Later: **self-efficacy**, **reciprocal determinism**.
- **Latent learning** and **cognitive maps** — E. C. Tolman (rats learn a maze without reward and show it when a reward appears).
- **Hull's drive-reduction** theory.

**Methods of practice** — the "uninterrupted and interrupted methods" are methods of **learning**: **massed** (continuous, uninterrupted) versus **spaced / distributed** (interrupted) practice. Spaced practice gives better retention. [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q080]] Other pairs: **whole vs part** method; **recitation** (active recall) vs passive reading.

**Transfer of learning:** positive (learning A helps B), negative (A hinders B), zero.

## 5. Yoga and the foundations of psychology

- Yoga is itself a psychology: the **Yoga Sūtra** defines yoga as the stilling of the mind's movements (*yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*, YS 1.2) and classifies the movements into five (*pramāṇa, viparyaya, vikalpa, nidrā, smṛti*, YS 1.6). *Pramāṇa* (valid knowing) matches perception and inference, *smṛti* is memory, *nidrā* is sleep.
- The Indian view of the inner instrument (*antaḥkaraṇa*: **manas, buddhi, citta, ahaṅkāra**) corresponds roughly to the mind's processing, judging, storing and self-referring functions.
- Saṃskāra (latent impressions) and vāsanā resemble learned dispositions and habits; *abhyāsa* (practice) and *vairāgya* (detachment), YS 1.12, are a theory of learning and unlearning.

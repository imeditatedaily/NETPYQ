# Nutrition III: Energy, Metabolism, BMI and the Balanced Diet

> **At a glance** — energy: carbohydrate **4**, protein **4**, fat **9**, alcohol 7 kcal/g · rich energy sources = **fats and carbohydrates** · **BMI = weight (kg) / height² (m²)** · Indian cut-offs: normal 18.5–22.9, overweight 23–24.9, obese ≥ 25 (WHO: obese ≥ 30) · energy cost: walking < swimming < running < climbing stairs; corporate officer < tailor < farmer < labourer · glycolysis → **pyruvic acid + ATP** · gluconeogenesis: glucose from **amino acids** (and glycerol of fats) · slow sugar release = **low-GI foods** · **2023 = International Year of Millets**; coarse grains promoted

Syllabus: "Concept of balanced diet, food groups; energy, BMR, SDA; metabolism." Also the six odd "diet" questions of December 2023 (Q146–150) belong here.

## 1. Energy in food

**Unit** — the **kilocalorie** (kcal, "Calorie"): heat needed to raise 1 kg of water by 1 °C; **1 kcal = 4.184 kJ**. Measured in a **bomb calorimeter**; the physiological values (**Atwater factors**) allow for digestion losses:

| Nutrient | kcal per gram |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** | **4** |
| **Protein** | **4** |
| **Fat** | **9** |
| Alcohol | 7 |
| Dietary fibre | ~2 |
| Vitamins, minerals, water | 0 |

The **rich sources of energy** are **fats and carbohydrates** (proteins are used for building first; vitamins give no energy). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q51]]

**Basal metabolic rate (BMR)** — the energy used **at complete physical and mental rest**, awake, lying down, 12–14 hours after the last meal (post-absorptive), at a comfortable temperature: the cost of keeping the heart, breathing, brain, kidneys and cell pumps going.
- About **1 kcal per kg per hour** in an adult man (~1,500–1,700 kcal/day); **60–70%** of total daily expenditure.
- **Higher** with: larger body surface area and lean mass, males, youth and growth, **thyroid hormones** (hyperthyroidism), **fever** (~12–13% per °C), pregnancy, cold climate, adrenaline, stress.
- **Lower** with: age, females, more fat, **starvation** and dieting, hypothyroidism, **sleep** (~10% lower).
- Measured by **indirect calorimetry**: O₂ consumed × ~4.8 kcal per litre of O₂. Meditation and yoga nidrā lower O₂ consumption below the waking resting level ("hypometabolic state"). [[LIKELY]]

**Respiratory quotient (RQ)** = CO₂ produced / O₂ consumed: **carbohydrate 1.0**, **protein ~0.8**, **fat ~0.7**, mixed diet ~0.82–0.85. [[LIKELY]]

**Specific dynamic action (SDA)** / **thermic effect of food** — the extra heat produced in digesting, absorbing and processing food: **protein ~20–30%** of its energy (the highest), carbohydrate ~5–10%, fat ~0–5%; for a mixed diet **~10%** of energy intake. [[LIKELY]]

**Total energy expenditure** = BMR + SDA + **physical activity**. ICMR-NIN (2020) sets energy needs by activity level (reference man 65 kg, woman 55 kg):

| Activity | Man (kcal/day) | Woman (kcal/day) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sedentary** | ~2,110 | ~1,660 | office workers, executives, teachers |
| **Moderate** | ~2,710 | ~2,130 | farmers, drivers, housewives with heavy chores |
| **Heavy** | ~3,470 | ~2,720 | labourers, stone cutters, rickshaw pullers |

Extra: pregnancy (+350 kcal in the 2nd and 3rd trimesters), lactation (+600 in the first 6 months).

- Increasing energy need by occupation: **corporate officer → tailor → farmer → labourer**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024JUN-P2-Q106]]
- Increasing energy cost per hour (70-kg man, Guyton's table): **walking** slowly ~200 kcal → **swimming** ~500 → **running** ~570 → **walking upstairs** ~1,100 (lifting the body against gravity). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q77]]

## 2. Metabolism

**Metabolism** — all the chemical reactions of the body: **anabolism** (building up, uses energy: protein synthesis, glycogenesis) and **catabolism** (breaking down, releases energy).

**Carbohydrate metabolism:**
- **Glycolysis** (Embden–Meyerhof pathway) — in the **cytoplasm**, without oxygen: **glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvic acid (3C)**, net **2 ATP** and 2 NADH. So its products include **pyruvic acid and ATP**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q78]] Without oxygen (hard exercise), pyruvate → **lactic acid** (muscle fatigue; the liver turns lactate back to glucose: **Cori cycle**).
- **Link reaction** — pyruvate → **acetyl-CoA** + CO₂ (in mitochondria).
- **Krebs (citric acid / TCA) cycle** — Hans Krebs, 1937; in the **mitochondrial matrix**; acetyl-CoA oxidised to CO₂, making NADH and FADH₂.
- **Electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation** — inner mitochondrial membrane; O₂ is the final electron acceptor (forms water); most ATP made here. Total ≈ **30–32 ATP per glucose** (older books: 36–38).
- **Glycogenesis** (glucose → glycogen; insulin) and **glycogenolysis** (glycogen → glucose; glucagon, adrenaline).
- **Gluconeogenesis** — making **new glucose from non-carbohydrate sources**, mainly in the **liver** (and kidney) during fasting: from **glucogenic amino acids** (alanine), **lactate** and **glycerol** (from fat). The key accepted "amino acids and fatty acids"; strictly, even-chain fatty acids cannot give net glucose in humans — only the glycerol part of fat can. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q38]]

**Fat metabolism**: **lipolysis** (triglyceride → glycerol + fatty acids), **β-oxidation** of fatty acids in mitochondria (→ acetyl-CoA), **ketogenesis** in the liver during starvation or uncontrolled diabetes (ketone bodies: acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone → ketoacidosis), lipogenesis.

**Protein metabolism**: deamination of amino acids; ammonia → **urea** in the liver (**urea cycle**, Krebs and Henseleit, 1932) → excreted by the kidneys; transamination.

**Hormonal control of blood glucose** (normal fasting 70–100 mg/dL): **insulin** lowers it (the only hormone that does; anabolic); **glucagon, adrenaline, cortisol, growth hormone, thyroxine** raise it.

**Diet and insulin sensitivity** — in diabetes, insulin sensitivity is improved by a diet **higher in protein and lower in (refined) carbohydrate**, with fibre and regular exercise and yoga. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q078]]

## 3. BMI, body composition and glycaemic index

**Body Mass Index (Quetelet index)** = **weight in kg ÷ (height in metres)²**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S43, YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q29]] Example: 70 kg, 1.75 m → 70 ÷ 3.06 ≈ 22.9.

| Category | WHO (international) | Asian / Indian cut-offs (WHO 2004; Indian consensus 2009) |
|---|---|---|
| Underweight | < 18.5 | < 18.5 |
| Normal | 18.5–24.9 | 18.5–22.9 |
| Overweight | 25–29.9 | 23–24.9 |
| Obese | ≥ 30 (I: 30–34.9, II: 35–39.9, III: ≥ 40) | ≥ 25 |

So BMI **22 and 24** denote **non-obese** (24 is normal by WHO, overweight but not obese by the Indian cut-offs); 26, 28 and 32 are obese by the Indian cut-offs. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q88]]

**Abdominal obesity** [[LIKELY]] — **waist circumference** > 90 cm (Indian men), > 80 cm (Indian women); waist–hip ratio > 0.9 (men), > 0.85 (women). Indians carry more body fat and visceral fat at the same BMI ("thin-fat Indian"), hence the lower cut-offs. Other measures: skinfold thickness, bioelectrical impedance, DEXA.

**Glycaemic index (GI)** (David Jenkins, 1981) — how fast a food raises blood glucose compared with glucose (= 100): **low ≤ 55**, medium 56–69, **high ≥ 70**. **Low-GI foods** release sugar slowly into the small intestine and blood (legumes, whole grains, millets, oats, most fruits, milk). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q072]] **Glycaemic load** = GI × available carbohydrate (g) ÷ 100. High-GI foods: white bread, white rice, potatoes, glucose, cornflakes. [[LIKELY]]

## 4. Balanced diet and food groups

**Balanced diet** — a diet that contains all nutrients **in the right amounts and proportions** for a person's **age, sex, physiological state and activity**, provides a small margin for lean periods, and keeps body weight healthy. When the diet does not meet a person's needs for age and work, the result is **under-nutrition**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q149]]

**Every yoga practitioner should particularly give importance to diet** (mitāhāra; see *Yogic and Āyurvedic Diet*). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q147]] The paper also asked the "only method to achieve holistic health" and keyed **yoga**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q146]]

**ICMR's five food groups** (classic):
1. **Cereals, millets and products** — energy, protein, B vitamins, fibre;
2. **Pulses and legumes** — protein, B vitamins, fibre;
3. **Milk, eggs, meat, fish** — protein, calcium, B12;
4. **Fruits and vegetables** — vitamins, minerals, fibre, antioxidants;
5. **Fats, oils and sugars** — energy, essential fatty acids.

**ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024)** [[LIKELY]] — "**My Plate for the Day**": about **half the plate vegetables and fruits** (with green leafy vegetables), the rest **cereals and millets**, **pulses / eggs / flesh foods**, **nuts and seeds**, **milk or curd**, with a small amount of fat and oil; at most ~45% of energy from cereals; limit **salt to 5 g a day**, limit added sugar, ultra-processed foods and fried foods; prefer whole grains and millets; drink enough water; read food labels; physical activity daily.

**Millets** (मोटा अनाज / "Shree Anna") — **2023 was declared the International Year of Millets** by the UN General Assembly at India's proposal (India had marked 2018 as the National Year of Millets and notified millets as "**nutri-cereals**"). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q148]] The government emphasises including **coarse grains** (millets) in the diet. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q150]]
- **Major millets**: **jowar** (sorghum), **bajra** (pearl millet — rich in iron), **ragi** (finger millet — richest in calcium and fibre).
- **Minor / small millets**: kodo, kutki (little millet), kangni (foxtail), sanwa (barnyard), cheena (proso), browntop.
- Benefits: low GI, gluten-free, high fibre and minerals, drought-hardy.

**Dietary planning for special groups** [[LIKELY]]: pregnancy and lactation (extra energy, protein, iron, folic acid, calcium), infants (exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months; colostrum rich in IgA), the elderly, athletes, diabetics (low GI, fibre, regular meals), hypertensives (DASH-type diet, low salt), obesity (energy deficit, fibre).

**Food safety and processing** [[LIKELY]]: fermentation (idli, curd) improves B vitamins and digestibility; sprouting raises vitamin C; parboiling keeps thiamine in rice; over-washing rice and discarding cooking water lose water-soluble vitamins; adulteration (FSSAI, 2006 Act).

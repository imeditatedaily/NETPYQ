# Yogic and Āyurvedic Diet

> **At a glance** — **mitāhāra**: sweet, unctuous food, leaving **a quarter** of the stomach empty (HP 1.60) · GS 5.22: **½ food, ¼ water, ¼ air** · Gītā: diet in **6.16–17** (moderation) and **17.7–10** (sāttvika, rājasa, tāmasa food) · tāmasa food = stale, tasteless, putrid, left over (17.10) · rājasa = pungent, sour, salty, very hot, sharp, dry (17.9) · kapha: favour **laghu, rūkṣa**; vāta: avoid **śīta, rūkṣa**; **guru** food suits **vāta and pitta** · **tikta and kaṣāya** pacify pitta (and kapha) · **madhura** ↓ vāta and pitta; **amla** ↓ vāta; **kaṭu** ↓ kapha; **kaṣāya** ↓ pitta and kapha · pitta has **40** nānātmaja disorders (vāta 80, kapha 20) · vāta: **caya in grīṣma, prakopa in varṣā, praśama in śarad**

The modern nutrition chapters describe *what* is in food; the yoga and Āyurveda texts describe *how much, when and in what state of mind* to eat. Both are in Unit 6's diet questions.

## 1. Mitāhāra in the haṭha texts

**Mitāhāra** (measured diet) is counted among the ten **yamas** in Haṭha Pradīpikā 1.17, and HP 1.40 calls it the most important of the yamas, as ahiṃsā is of the niyamas.

**Definition** (Haṭha Pradīpikā 1.60 in our edition): *susnigdha-madhurāhāraś caturthāṃśa-vivarjitaḥ | bhujyate śiva-samprītyai mitāhāraḥ sa ucyate* — "Food that is pleasantly **unctuous and sweet**, leaving **one quarter** of the stomach empty, eaten as an offering to please Śiva: that is called mitāhāra." The same verse is found in the Gorakṣa Śataka and the Yoga Kuṇḍalī and Yoga Cūḍāmaṇi Upaniṣads.

**Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā** (chapter 5, before prāṇāyāma):
- **5.16** — one who begins yoga **without mitāhāra** gets various diseases and no success.
- **5.17** — recommended: rice, barley or wheat preparations, mung, black gram, chickpea and similar, clean and husked.
- **5.21** — pure, sweet, unctuous food, leaving **half** the stomach empty.
- **5.22** — *annena pūrayed ardhaṃ toyena tu tṛtīyakam | udarasya turīyāṃśaṃ saṃrakṣed vāyu-cāraṇe* — **fill half the stomach with food, the third quarter with water, and keep the fourth quarter free for the movement of air**.
- **5.30** — avoid hard, spoiled, putrid, very hot, stale, very cold food.

**Pathya and apathya** (Haṭha Pradīpikā):
- **Apathya** (1.61, avoid): bitter-pungent, sour, sharp, salty and hot foods; green leafy vegetables of certain kinds; sour gruel; oil, sesame, mustard; alcohol; fish; goat and other meat; curd, buttermilk; horse gram (*kulattha*), jujube, oil-cake, asafoetida, garlic. Also food that is reheated, dry, over-salted or sour. Avoid **fire, women and long journeys** in the early stage of practice.
- **Pathya** (1.65, wholesome): **wheat, rice, barley, ṣaṣṭika rice**, milk, ghee, brown sugar, butter, sugar candy, honey, dry ginger, paṭola (pointed gourd), the five vegetables, **mung** and pure water.

## 2. Diet in the Bhagavad Gītā and the Upaniṣads

**Gītā 6.16–17** — moderation: *nātyaśnatas tu yogo'sti na caikāntam anaśnataḥ* — "yoga is not for one who **eats too much**, nor for one who does not eat at all, nor for one who sleeps too much or too little"; *yuktāhāra-vihārasya yukta-ceṣṭasya karmasu | yukta-svapnāvabodhasya yogo bhavati duḥkhahā* — "for one **moderate in food and recreation**, in effort at work, in sleep and waking, yoga destroys sorrow."

**Gītā 17.7–10** — food classified by the guṇas (*āhāras tv api sarvasya trividho bhavati priyaḥ*, 17.7):

| Food | Gītā | Description | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sāttvika** | 17.8 | promotes life, purity, strength, health, joy and cheerfulness; **juicy (rasya), unctuous (snigdha), substantial (sthira), agreeable (hṛdya)** | calm, clarity |
| **Rājasa** | 17.9 | **pungent (kaṭu), sour (amla), salty (lavaṇa), very hot, sharp (tīkṣṇa), dry (rūkṣa), burning (vidāhin)** | pain, grief and disease |
| **Tāmasa** | 17.10 | ***yātayāma*** (stale, kept over a *yāma* — or half-cooked), ***gatarasa*** (tasteless), ***pūti*** (putrid), ***paryuṣita*** (left overnight), ***ucchiṣṭa*** (leftovers of others), ***amedhya*** (impure) | dullness, inertia |

- The verses of the Gītā on diet among the options were **6.17** and chapter **17** (keyed 17/4; strictly the food verses are 17.7–10). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q066]]
- Food dear to the **tāmasa** person: **half-cooked (stale), tasteless, left over** (17.10). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q117]]
- **Rājasa food is not beneficial for a yoga practitioner**, because **pungent, salty and sharp food increases rajas**, which obstructs practice. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q66]]

**Upaniṣads**: *annād bhavanti bhūtāni* (beings arise from food, Gītā 3.14); *annam brahmeti vyajānāt* (Bhṛgu knew food as Brahman, Taittirīya 3.2); the **annamaya kośa** (Taittirīya 2.1); "the mind is made of food" — *annamayaṃ hi somya manaḥ* (Chāndogya 6.5.4); *āhāra-śuddhau sattva-śuddhiḥ, sattva-śuddhau dhruvā smṛtiḥ* — "when food is pure, the mind becomes pure; when the mind is pure, memory becomes firm" (Chāndogya 7.26.2). ## 3. Āyurveda: doṣas, guṇas and diet by constitution

**Tridoṣa** — **vāta** (ākāśa + vāyu: movement), **pitta** (agni + jala: transformation, digestion), **kapha** (pṛthvī + jala: structure, stability). **Prakṛti** (constitution) is set at conception by the predominant doṣa(s).

**Qualities of the doṣas** (Caraka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 1.59–61):
- **Vāta**: rūkṣa (dry), **śīta** (cold), **laghu** (light), sūkṣma (subtle), cala (mobile), viśada (clear), khara (rough).
- **Pitta**: sasneha (slightly oily), **uṣṇa** (hot), **tīkṣṇa** (sharp), drava (liquid), amla (sour), sara (flowing), kaṭu (pungent), **laghu** (light).
- **Kapha**: **guru** (heavy), śīta (cold), mṛdu (soft), **snigdha** (unctuous), madhura (sweet), sthira (stable), picchila (slimy).

**Principle** — like increases like; **opposites pacify** (*sāmānya–viśeṣa siddhānta*).

| Constitution | Beneficial food | Harmful food |
|---|---|---|
| **Vāta** | warm, unctuous (*snigdha*), heavy (*guru*), moist; sweet, sour, salty | **cold (*śīta*) and dry (*rūkṣa*)**, light; pungent, bitter, astringent |
| **Pitta** | cool, heavy, slightly oily; sweet, bitter, astringent | hot, sharp, sour, salty, pungent; fried |
| **Kapha** | **light (*laghu*), dry (*rūkṣa*)**, warm; pungent, bitter, astringent | heavy, unctuous, cold; sweet, sour, salty |

- For **kapha** prakṛti the beneficial kinds of food are **laghu and rūkṣa** (light and dry). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q18]]
- Food **not** beneficial for **vāta** constitution: **śīta (cold) and rūkṣa (dry)**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q45]]
- Food predominant in **guru guṇa** (heavy) suits **both vātaja and pittaja** prakṛti (opposite to their lightness) and aggravates kapha. [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q091]]

**Nānātmaja vikāra** (disorders caused by one doṣa alone; Caraka Sūtrasthāna 20): **vāta 80**, **pitta 40**, **kapha 20**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q28]] (Sāmānyaja vikāras arise from more than one doṣa.)

[[LIKELY]] The twenty guṇas (*gurvādi guṇa*: guru–laghu, śīta–uṣṇa, snigdha–rūkṣa, manda–tīkṣṇa, sthira–sara, mṛdu–kaṭhina, viśada–picchila, ślakṣṇa–khara, sūkṣma–sthūla, sāndra–drava); the seats of the doṣas; the five types of each doṣa (e.g. prāṇa, udāna, samāna, vyāna, apāna vāta).

## 4. The six rasas (tastes)

| Rasa | Elements (mahābhūta) | Pacifies (↓) | Aggravates (↑) | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Madhura** (sweet) | pṛthvī + jala | **vāta, pitta** | kapha | rice, wheat, milk, ghee, sugar |
| **Amla** (sour) | pṛthvī + agni | **vāta** | pitta, kapha | lemon, curd, tamarind |
| **Lavaṇa** (salty) | jala + agni | vāta | pitta, kapha | rock salt |
| **Kaṭu** (pungent) | vāyu + agni | **kapha** | vāta, pitta | chilli, pepper, ginger |
| **Tikta** (bitter) | vāyu + ākāśa | **pitta, kapha** | vāta | neem, bitter gourd |
| **Kaṣāya** (astringent) | vāyu + pṛthvī | **pitta, kapha** | vāta | unripe banana, pomegranate rind, lentils |

Caraka Sūtrasthāna 1.66 in one line: **sweet, sour and salty conquer vāta; astringent, sweet and bitter conquer pitta; astringent, pungent and bitter conquer kapha.**

- **Madhura → reduces vāta and pitta; amla → reduces vāta; kaṭu → reduces kapha; kaṣāya → reduces pitta and kapha.** [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P2-Q44]]
- The tastes that pacify **pitta**: **tikta (bitter) and kaṣāya (astringent)** (kaṭu and lavaṇa raise it). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q56]]
- Food predominant in **kaṣāya and tikta** suits both **pittaja and kaphaja** prakṛti. [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q115]]
- Food predominant in **pungent, bitter and astringent** tastes is not good for **vāta** constitution, because eating too much of them **increases vāta**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q67]]

Other properties of a substance (*dravya*): **guṇa**, **vīrya** (potency: uṣṇa or śīta), **vipāka** (post-digestive taste: madhura, amla or kaṭu), and **prabhāva** (special action). [[LIKELY]]

## 5. Seasons, rules of eating and wholesome habits

**Six ṛtus** — śiśira (late winter), vasanta (spring), grīṣma (summer) = **ādāna kāla** (the sun draws away strength; northern course); varṣā (rains), śarad (autumn), hemanta (early winter) = **visarga kāla** (the moon gives strength).

**Seasonal cycle of the doṣas** — accumulation (*caya*), aggravation (*prakopa*), pacification (*praśama*) (Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Sūtrasthāna 12):

| Doṣa | Caya (accumulates) | Prakopa (aggravated) | Praśama (subsides) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vāta** | **grīṣma** | **varṣā** | **śarad** |
| Pitta | varṣā | śarad | hemanta |
| Kapha | śiśira | vasanta | grīṣma |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q075]] This is why **virecana** (purgation) is done in śarad for pitta, **vamana** in vasanta for kapha, and **basti** in varṣā for vāta. [[LIKELY]]

**Rules of eating** (Caraka Vimānasthāna 1 — the eight factors of diet, *aṣṭa āhāra-vidhi-viśeṣāyatana*): **prakṛti** (nature of the food), **karaṇa** (processing), **saṃyoga** (combination), **rāśi** (quantity), **deśa** (place/habitat), **kāla** (time and season), **upayoga-saṃsthā** (rules of use), **upayoktā** (the eater). Also: eat warm, unctuous food in proper quantity, after the previous meal is digested, without incompatible combinations, in a pleasant place, not too fast or too slow, without talking or laughing, with attention.

**Trayopastambha** — the three sub-pillars of life: **āhāra** (food), **nidrā** (sleep), **brahmacarya** (regulated conduct) (Caraka Sūtrasthāna 11.35). **Viruddha āhāra** (incompatible food): milk with fish or sour fruits, honey heated or mixed with equal ghee, etc. **Agni** (digestive fire) — sama, viṣama (vāta), tīkṣṇa (pitta), manda (kapha); **āma** (undigested toxic residue) when agni is weak.

**Yogic diet in practice** — mostly sāttvika, lacto-vegetarian, fresh and lightly cooked; eat at regular times; a gap of ~3–4 hours after a meal before āsana and prāṇāyāma; fasting (*upavāsa*) occasionally; water before rather than with heavy meals; gratitude and calm while eating. Dec 2023 asked that every yoga practitioner should give particular importance to **diet** (see *Nutrition III*).

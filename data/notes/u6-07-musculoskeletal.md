# Musculoskeletal System: Bones, Joints and Muscles

> **At a glance** — **206** bones: axial **80**, appendicular **126** · vertebrae: **7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral (fused), 4 coccygeal (fused)** · bone shapes: long (arms, legs), short (wrist), flat (skull, sternum, scapula), irregular (vertebrae) · ear ossicles: malleus, incus, **stapes** (smallest) · wrist: scaphoid, lunate, capitate… · synovial joints are **freely movable**; pivot joint at the **elbow** (proximal radio-ulnar) and atlanto-axial · contraction = **actin–myosin**; functional unit = **sarcomere** · shoulder muscle = **deltoid**

Syllabus: "Musculoskeletal system: bones, joints and muscles; neuromuscular junction; sarcotubular system; excitation–contraction coupling; smooth muscle." This topic has given 22+ questions — the joint-top of Unit 6 biology. Asana practice makes it doubly important.

## 1. The skeleton: 206 bones

**Functions of bone**: support, protection (skull–brain, ribs–heart and lungs), movement (levers for muscles), **blood-cell formation** (red marrow), mineral store (99% of the body's calcium, phosphate), fat store (yellow marrow).

**Bone tissue** — compact (dense; **Haversian systems / osteons**) and spongy (cancellous; trabeculae with red marrow). Cells: **osteoblasts** (build), **osteocytes** (maintain), **osteoclasts** (resorb). A long bone has a **diaphysis** (shaft), **epiphyses** (ends), and the **epiphyseal plate** (growth plate) between them; covered by **periosteum**.

**Axial skeleton — 80 bones**:

| Part | Number |
|---|---|
| Skull: cranium 8 + face 14 | 22 |
| **Ear ossicles** (malleus, incus, stapes × 2) | 6 |
| **Hyoid** | 1 |
| **Vertebral column** (adult) | 26 |
| **Sternum** | 1 |
| **Ribs** (12 pairs) | 24 |

**Appendicular skeleton — 126 bones**:

| Part | Number |
|---|---|
| Pectoral girdle: **clavicle** × 2, **scapula** × 2 | 4 |
| Upper limbs: humerus, radius, ulna, 8 carpals, 5 metacarpals, 14 phalanges (× 2) | 60 |
| Pelvic girdle: hip bones (each = ilium + ischium + pubis fused) | 2 |
| Lower limbs: femur, **patella**, tibia, **fibula**, 7 tarsals, 5 metatarsals, 14 phalanges (× 2) | 60 |

So the **axial** skeleton includes the **vertebrae, skull and hyoid** (clavicle and scapula are appendicular) [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S33]]; and the bones **not** in the appendicular skeleton are the **cervical vertebrae and sternum** (clavicle, scapula and pelvis are appendicular). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q103]]

A newborn has about **270–300** bones; many fuse. Largest bone: **femur**; smallest: **stapes**.

**Vertebral column** — 33 vertebrae in a child, **26** in an adult:

| Region | Number | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cervical** | **7** | C1 **atlas** (holds the skull; "yes" nod), C2 **axis** (dens; "no" rotation); transverse foramina |
| **Thoracic** | **12** | articulate with the 12 pairs of ribs |
| **Lumbar** | **5** | largest bodies, bear most weight |
| **Sacral** | **5** fused → 1 sacrum | |
| **Coccygeal** | **4** fused → 1 coccyx | |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q088, YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q72]] From top to bottom: **cervical → thoracic → lumbar → sacral**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q76]]

**Spinal curves**: cervical and lumbar **lordosis** (secondary, concave backwards), thoracic and sacral **kyphosis** (primary). Abnormal: exaggerated kyphosis (hunchback), lordosis (sway back), **scoliosis** (lateral curve). [[LIKELY]] **Intervertebral discs**: outer annulus fibrosus, inner nucleus pulposus (slipped disc = herniated nucleus pulposus).

## 3. Joints and movements

**Classification of joints (arthroses):**

| Structural | Functional | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fibrous** | **synarthrosis** (immovable) | **sutures** of the skull, teeth in sockets |
| **Cartilaginous** | **amphiarthrosis** (slightly movable) | pubic symphysis, **intervertebral discs** |
| **Synovial** | **diarthrosis** (**freely movable**) | most limb joints |

**All synovial joints are freely movable.** [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q35]] A synovial joint has a **joint capsule**, **synovial membrane** secreting **synovial fluid** (lubrication, nutrition), **articular (hyaline) cartilage** on the bone ends, and ligaments.

**Six types of synovial joint:**

| Type | Movement | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Plane / gliding** | sliding | intercarpal, intertarsal, facet joints of vertebrae |
| **Hinge** | flexion–extension (one axis) | **elbow** (humero-ulnar), knee, ankle, finger joints |
| **Pivot** | rotation (one axis) | **atlanto-axial** (head turning "no"), **proximal radio-ulnar** at the **elbow** (pronation–supination) |
| **Condyloid / ellipsoid** | two axes | **wrist** (radio-carpal), knuckles (MCP) |
| **Saddle** | two axes, more freedom | carpo-metacarpal joint of the **thumb** |
| **Ball-and-socket** | all axes | **shoulder**, **hip** |

The **pivot joint** asked about was the **elbow** (proximal radio-ulnar joint); shoulder and hip are ball-and-socket, the wrist condyloid. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q24]] Types of joint include **gliding, condylar and saddle**; inversion and protraction are **movements**, not joints. [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q099]]

**Movements at joints** (every asana is a combination of these):

| Movement | Meaning | Asana example |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexion** | decreasing the angle between parts | knee to chest (pavanamuktāsana); forward bends |
| **Extension** / hyperextension | increasing the angle | backbends (bhujaṅgāsana) |
| **Abduction** | moving away from the midline | leg out to the side |
| **Adduction** | moving towards the midline | bringing it back |
| Rotation (medial/lateral) | turning on the long axis | twists, head rotation |
| Circumduction | circular movement (cone) | shoulder circles |
| Pronation / supination | palm down / palm up | — |
| Dorsiflexion / plantarflexion | toes up / toes down | ankle movements |
| **Inversion / eversion** | sole inward / outward | — |
| **Protraction / retraction** | forward / backward (jaw, scapula) | — |
| Elevation / depression | up / down (shoulders) | — |

- Lifting the **knee towards the chest** is **flexion** (of the hip and knee). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q41]]
- Lifting the straight leg out to the side and bringing it back = **abduction** then **adduction**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q104]]

## 4. Muscles: types and the main skeletal muscles

| | **Skeletal** | **Cardiac** | **Smooth** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Striations | striated | striated | **non-striated** (unstriped) |
| Control | **voluntary** | involuntary | **involuntary** |
| Cells | long, cylindrical, **multinucleate** | branched, 1–2 nuclei, **intercalated discs** (gap junctions) | spindle-shaped, one nucleus |
| Where | attached to bones; **tongue**, **pharynx**, **upper part of the oesophagus**, external sphincters, diaphragm | heart only | walls of hollow organs: gut, blood vessels, airways, bladder, uterus, iris |
| Fatigue | fatigues | does not fatigue (long refractory period) | slow, sustained (tone) |

- **Voluntary muscle** is found in the **tongue**, the **beginning of the oesophagus** and the **pharynx**; the lower oesophagus is smooth, and the dermis has no voluntary muscle (its arrector pili are smooth). [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q112]]
- **Smooth muscles** are found in **all involuntary organs except the heart**, and in the **intestine** (and the liver's vessels and ducts); not in the heart (cardiac) or in the limbs (skeletal). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q49]]

**Smooth muscle types** [[LIKELY]]: **single-unit (visceral)** — cells joined by gap junctions, contract together (gut, uterus, small vessels); **multi-unit** — each fibre separately innervated (iris, ciliary muscle, arrector pili, large arteries). Contraction is triggered by Ca²⁺ binding **calmodulin**, which activates myosin light-chain kinase (no troponin).

**Main skeletal muscles** (there are about 640):

| Muscle | Where / action |
|---|---|
| **Deltoid** | **shoulder**; abducts the arm [[PYQ: YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q21]] |
| Trapezius | upper back; elevates/retracts the scapula |
| Pectoralis major | chest; adducts the arm |
| Latissimus dorsi | back; extends and adducts the arm |
| Biceps brachii / triceps brachii | front / back of upper arm; flex / extend the elbow |
| **Diaphragm** | main muscle of breathing (phrenic nerve, C3–C5) |
| Rectus abdominis, obliques, transversus abdominis | abdominal wall; flexion of trunk, forced expiration, **uḍḍiyāna** and **nauli** use them |
| Erector spinae | along the spine; extends the back |
| Iliopsoas | hip flexor |
| **Gluteus maximus** | buttock; extends the hip — **largest** muscle |
| Quadriceps femoris | front of thigh; extends the knee |
| Hamstrings | back of thigh; flex the knee, extend the hip (stretched in paścimottānāsana) |
| **Sartorius** | thigh — **longest** muscle |
| Gastrocnemius, soleus | calf; plantarflex the ankle |
| Masseter | jaw — among the strongest |
| **Stapedius** | middle ear — **smallest** muscle |

## 5. How muscle contracts

**Structure of a skeletal muscle** — muscle → fascicles (bundles, wrapped in perimysium) → **muscle fibres** (cells, in endomysium) → **myofibrils** → **myofilaments**: thick (**myosin**) and thin (**actin**, with **tropomyosin** and **troponin**). The whole muscle is wrapped in epimysium.

**Sarcomere** — the **smallest functional (contractile) unit** of a muscle, from one **Z line** to the next (~2–2.5 µm): [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q083]]
- **A band** (dark, *anisotropic*) = length of the myosin filaments;
- **I band** (light, *isotropic*) = actin only; shortens in contraction;
- **H zone** = myosin only; shortens;
- **M line** = middle of the sarcomere.

**Sliding filament theory** (H. E. Huxley and J. Hanson; A. F. Huxley and R. Niedergerke; 1954): the **actin–myosin filaments** slide over each other — the structure responsible for contraction; the filaments themselves do not shorten. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q073]]

**Neuromuscular junction (NMJ)** [[LIKELY]]: a motor neuron's impulse releases **acetylcholine (ACh)** → ACh binds **nicotinic receptors** on the motor end plate → **end-plate potential** → muscle action potential. **Acetylcholinesterase** destroys ACh. Disorders and drugs: **myasthenia gravis** (antibodies against ACh receptors: weakness, drooping eyelids), **curare** (blocks receptors), botulinum toxin (blocks ACh release). A **motor unit** = one motor neuron + all the fibres it supplies.

**Sarcotubular system and excitation–contraction coupling** [[LIKELY]]:
1. The muscle action potential runs along the sarcolemma and down the **T-tubules** (transverse tubules).
2. At the **triad** (one T-tubule between two terminal cisternae of the **sarcoplasmic reticulum**), it opens Ca²⁺ channels (ryanodine receptors).
3. **Ca²⁺** floods out and binds **troponin C**.
4. Troponin moves **tropomyosin** off the binding sites on actin.
5. **Myosin heads** bind actin (cross-bridges), **power stroke**, pulling actin towards the M line.
6. **ATP** binds myosin → head detaches → ATP hydrolysed → head re-cocks.
7. Relaxation: Ca²⁺ pumped back into the SR (also uses ATP).

Without ATP the heads cannot detach: **rigor mortis**.

**Types of contraction**: **isotonic** (length changes: concentric — shortening, eccentric — lengthening under load) and **isometric** (tension without length change: holding an asana). **Muscle fibre types**: type I (slow-twitch, red, aerobic, fatigue-resistant: posture) and type II (fast-twitch, white, anaerobic: sprinting). **Muscle tone**, **stretch reflex** (muscle spindle), **Golgi tendon organ** (protects against excessive tension — the basis of relaxation after holding a stretch). **Energy for contraction**: ATP, creatine phosphate, glycolysis (lactic acid), aerobic oxidation.

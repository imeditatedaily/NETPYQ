# Cardiovascular and Respiratory Systems

> **At a glance** — **SA node** in the **right atrium** = pacemaker → atria → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres → ventricles · **tricuspid** valve: right atrium ↔ right ventricle; bicuspid (mitral): left · **pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood** · cardiac cycle **0.8 s** · BP 120/80 mmHg · airway: trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli · **tidal volume** ≈ 500 mL · **TLC = VC + RV**; **FRC = ERV + RV** · respiratory centres in the **medulla and pons**; voluntary control (prāṇāyāma) by the **cerebral cortex**

Syllabus: "Cardiovascular system: heart, conducting system, cardiac cycle, blood pressure and its regulation" and "Respiratory system: mechanics of breathing, pulmonary ventilation, regulation of respiration, transport of gases, hypoxia." For a yoga student these are the physiology of prāṇāyāma.

## 1. The heart and the circulation

**Heart**: ~250–350 g, size of a fist, in the **mediastinum** between the lungs, apex to the left; covered by the **pericardium**; wall = epicardium, **myocardium** (cardiac muscle), endocardium. **Four chambers**: two **atria** (auricles; receiving) and two **ventricles** (pumping; left wall ~3× thicker).

**Valves** (keep flow one-way):
- **Tricuspid** — between the **right atrium and right ventricle** [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q23]]
- **Bicuspid (mitral)** — between the left atrium and left ventricle
- **Pulmonary semilunar** — right ventricle → pulmonary artery
- **Aortic semilunar** — left ventricle → aorta
- *Chordae tendineae* and papillary muscles stop the AV valves turning inside out.

**Path of blood** (William Harvey described the circulation, 1628):

body → **superior and inferior venae cavae** → **right atrium** → **tricuspid valve** → right ventricle → pulmonary valve → **pulmonary artery** (deoxygenated) → **lungs** → **pulmonary veins** (oxygenated) → **left atrium** → **bicuspid valve** → left ventricle → aortic valve → **aorta** → body.

- Order asked: **right auricle → tricuspid valve → pulmonary artery ("pulmonary aorta") → pulmonary veins ("pulmonary vena cava") → bicuspid valve**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q132]]
- **Deoxygenated blood flows in the pulmonary artery** — the only adult artery carrying deoxygenated blood (the umbilical arteries do so in the fetus); the pulmonary veins are the only veins carrying oxygenated blood. [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q083]]

**Circulations**: **pulmonary** (right heart → lungs → left heart) and **systemic** (left heart → body → right heart); special: coronary (heart's own supply; blockage → myocardial infarction), hepatic portal (gut → liver), cerebral.

**Blood vessels**: **arteries** (thick muscular elastic walls, away from the heart, high pressure), **arterioles** (resistance vessels: control BP), **capillaries** (one cell thick: exchange), **venules**, **veins** (thin walls, **valves**, low pressure; hold ~60–70% of blood volume; muscle pump of the legs returns blood — inversions assist venous return).

## 2. Conducting system, cardiac cycle and blood pressure

**Conducting system** — cardiac muscle is **myogenic** (makes its own beat):
1. **Sino-atrial (SA) node** — in the wall of the **right atrium** near the opening of the superior vena cava; the **pacemaker**, ~70–80 impulses/min. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q23]]
2. Impulse spreads across both **atria** → atrial contraction.
3. **Atrio-ventricular (AV) node** — delays the impulse ~0.1 s so the atria finish emptying.
4. **Bundle of His** → right and left **bundle branches** in the septum.
5. **Purkinje fibres** spread the impulse through the ventricular myocardium (right and left) → ventricular contraction.

Order asked: **SA node activity → stimulus reaches AV node (atrial contraction) → spread through Purkinje fibres on the right ventricle → impulses reach the ventricular myocardium → atrial contraction complete and ventricular contraction begins**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q099]]

**ECG** (Willem Einthoven, 1903) [[LIKELY]]: **P wave** = atrial depolarisation; **QRS complex** = ventricular depolarisation (atrial repolarisation hidden in it); **T wave** = ventricular repolarisation; PR interval ~0.12–0.2 s (AV conduction).

**Cardiac cycle** [[LIKELY]] — at 75 beats/min it lasts **0.8 s**: atrial systole 0.1 s, ventricular systole 0.3 s, diastole 0.4 s (joint diastole). **Heart sounds**: **S1 "lub"** — closure of the AV (tricuspid, mitral) valves at the start of ventricular systole; **S2 "dub"** — closure of the semilunar valves at the start of diastole.

**Cardiac output** = stroke volume × heart rate ≈ 70 mL × 72/min ≈ **5 L/min**. **Frank–Starling law**: the more the heart is filled (stretched) in diastole, the more forcefully it contracts. Heart rate: normal 60–100/min; bradycardia < 60 (common in trained athletes and long-term yoga practitioners), tachycardia > 100.

**Blood pressure** — the pressure of blood on the arterial walls: **systolic ~120 mmHg / diastolic ~80 mmHg**; **pulse pressure** = 40; **mean arterial pressure** ≈ diastolic + ⅓ pulse pressure ≈ 93 mmHg. Measured with a **sphygmomanometer** (Riva-Rocci, 1896) using **Korotkoff sounds** (1905). BP = cardiac output × peripheral resistance.

**Regulation of BP** [[LIKELY]]:
- **Short-term (nervous)**: **baroreceptors** in the **carotid sinus** and **aortic arch** sense stretch → **vasomotor and cardiac centres in the medulla** → if BP rises, vagal slowing of the heart and vasodilation (baroreflex). Slow breathing and some inversions stimulate the baroreflex.
- **Hormonal / long-term**: **renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system** (kidney renin → angiotensin II: vasoconstriction, aldosterone, thirst), **ADH**, **ANP** (from the atria: lowers BP), adrenaline.
- **Hypertension**: ≥ 140/90 mmHg (WHO/ISH and Indian guidelines; 130/80 in the 2017 American guideline). Yoga, slow breathing and meditation lower BP modestly.

## 3. The respiratory tract and its functions

**Airway in order**: nose (filters, warms, moistens; **smell**) → **pharynx** → **larynx** (voice box: **vocal cords**; epiglottis) → **trachea** (C-shaped cartilage rings, ciliated epithelium) → **right and left bronchi** → **bronchioles** → terminal and respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → **alveoli** (~300–480 million; ~70 m² surface; one-cell-thick walls; **type I** cells for exchange, **type II** cells make **surfactant**, which stops alveoli collapsing).

Order asked: **trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P2-Q40]]

**Functions of the respiratory system**: **ventilation** and gas exchange, **smell** (olfaction), **creation of sound** (phonation in the larynx), acid–base balance (CO₂ removal), defence (cilia, mucus, macrophages), venous return (thoracic pump), and making angiotensin II (ACE in the lungs). Vision is not one. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q35]]

**Lungs** — right lung **3 lobes**, left **2 lobes** (cardiac notch); covered by the **pleura** (visceral and parietal; pleural fluid).

## 4. Mechanics of breathing and lung volumes

**Inspiration** (active):
- **Diaphragm** contracts and flattens (does ~**75%** of quiet breathing; nerve: **phrenic**, C3–C5).
- **External intercostals** lift the ribs up and out (bucket-handle and pump-handle movements).
- Thoracic volume ↑ → intrapulmonary pressure falls below atmospheric → air flows in (Boyle's law).
- In forced inspiration: sternocleidomastoid, scalenes, pectoralis minor.

**Expiration**: quiet expiration is **passive** (elastic recoil); **forced** expiration uses the **internal intercostals** and **abdominal muscles** (rectus abdominis, obliques, transversus) — as in **kapālabhāti**.

The inhalation sequence keyed in one paper: **short intercostals → external intercostals → internal (interchondral) intercostals → abdominal muscles → rectus abdominis** (the abdominal wall relaxing to let the diaphragm descend). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q103]] Remember for any question that the **diaphragm** is the chief muscle of quiet inspiration.

**Lung volumes and capacities** (healthy young adult male; Guyton):

| Volume / capacity | Definition | Value |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | air breathed in or out in one **normal, quiet** breath | **~500 mL** |
| Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV) | extra air that can be inhaled after a normal inspiration | ~3,000 mL |
| Expiratory reserve volume (ERV) | extra air that can be exhaled after a normal expiration | ~1,100 mL |
| Residual volume (RV) | air left after maximal expiration (cannot be exhaled) | ~1,200 mL |
| Inspiratory capacity (IC) | TV + IRV | ~3,500 mL |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | **ERV + RV** (left after a normal expiration) | ~2,300 mL |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | IRV + TV + ERV (maximum air exhaled after maximum inhalation) | ~4,600 mL |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | **VC + RV** (= IRV + TV + ERV + RV) | ~5,800 mL |

- The volume moved in and out in normal breathing is the **tidal volume**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S13]]
- **Total lung capacity** = **vital capacity + residual volume**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q55]]
- **Functional residual capacity** = **expiratory reserve volume + residual volume**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q100]]

**Ventilation** [[LIKELY]]: **minute ventilation** = TV × rate = 500 mL × 12 ≈ **6 L/min**; **anatomical dead space** ≈ **150 mL** (air in the conducting airways that never reaches the alveoli); **alveolar ventilation** = (500 − 150) × 12 ≈ **4.2 L/min**. Slow, deep breathing (as in prāṇāyāma) improves alveolar ventilation for the same minute volume because less of each breath is dead space. Volumes are measured with a **spirometer** (RV, FRC and TLC need gas dilution or body plethysmography). Normal respiratory rate 12–16/min; yoga practitioners often breathe 6–8/min at rest.

## 5. Regulation of breathing, gas transport and hypoxia

**Respiratory centres** — groups of neurons in the **medulla oblongata** and **pons varolii** (not the cerebellum or midbrain): [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q113]]
- **Medulla**: **dorsal respiratory group** (inspiration; basic rhythm) and **ventral respiratory group** (forced breathing); the pre-Bötzinger complex is the rhythm generator.
- **Pons**: **pneumotaxic centre** (limits inspiration, sets rate) and **apneustic centre** (prolongs inspiration).

**Chemical control**: **central chemoreceptors** in the medulla respond to **CO₂ / H⁺** in CSF (the strongest everyday drive); **peripheral chemoreceptors** in the **carotid and aortic bodies** respond mainly to low **O₂** (and CO₂, H⁺). **Hering–Breuer inflation reflex** (1868): lung stretch receptors stop over-inflation via the vagus.

**Voluntary control** — breathing can be overridden at will by the **cerebral cortex** (motor cortex → corticospinal tract → respiratory muscles, bypassing the medulla): this is how **prāṇāyāma**, breath-holding (kumbhaka), speech and singing control the breath. [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q133]] Breath-holding ends at the "breaking point" when CO₂ rises; training (and the calm of practice) raises CO₂ tolerance.

**Gas exchange** — by **diffusion** across the respiratory membrane, down partial-pressure gradients: alveolar PO₂ ~100 mmHg vs venous 40; venous PCO₂ ~46 vs alveolar 40.

**Transport of oxygen** [[LIKELY]]: ~**98.5%** bound to **haemoglobin** (oxyhaemoglobin; 1 g Hb carries ~1.34 mL O₂), ~1.5% dissolved. The **oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve** is **sigmoid**; it shifts **right** (O₂ released more easily) with ↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺ (↓ pH), ↑ temperature, ↑ 2,3-BPG — the **Bohr effect** (Christian Bohr, 1904).

**Transport of carbon dioxide**: ~**70% as bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻, via carbonic anhydrase in RBCs; **chloride shift**), ~**23% as carbaminohaemoglobin**, ~**7% dissolved**. **Haldane effect**: deoxygenated blood carries more CO₂.

**Hypoxia** (lack of O₂ at the tissues) — four types:
1. **Hypoxic** (hypoxaemic): low PO₂ in arterial blood — high altitude, lung disease;
2. **Anaemic**: too little functional haemoglobin — anaemia, **carbon monoxide** poisoning;
3. **Stagnant** (circulatory): slow blood flow — heart failure, shock;
4. **Histotoxic**: cells cannot use O₂ — **cyanide** poisoning.

Related terms: **cyanosis** (bluish skin; > 5 g/dL deoxygenated Hb), **hypercapnia** (excess CO₂), **apnoea** (no breathing), **dyspnoea** (difficult breathing), **hyperventilation** (washes out CO₂ → alkalosis, tingling, dizziness).

**Prāṇāyāma physiology** (link questions) — slow breathing improves ventilation–perfusion matching, raises vagal tone and heart-rate variability (respiratory sinus arrhythmia), lowers BP; kumbhaka raises CO₂ and trains chemoreceptor tolerance; kapālabhāti is active, forced expiration with passive inspiration; ujjāyī adds airway resistance and lengthens the breath.

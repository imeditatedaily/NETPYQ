# Digestive and Excretory Systems

> **At a glance** — largest gland = **liver** · parotid duct = **Stensen's (Stenson's) duct** · small intestine = **duodenum, jejunum, ileum** · large intestine = **caecum (with the vermiform appendix) → colon → rectum → anal canal** · steps: **ingestion → digestion → absorption → assimilation → defecation** · difficulty swallowing = **dysphagia** · ulcer in the first part of the small intestine = **duodenal ulcer** · jaundice = raised **serum bilirubin** · urinary tract: **kidney → ureter → bladder → urethra** · nephron: glomerulus → PCT → loop of Henle → DCT → collecting duct · GFR ≈ 125 mL/min; regulated by **angiotensin II and ANP**

Syllabus: "Digestive and excretory system: GI secretions, motility, GI hormones; renal physiology: nephron, GFR, urine concentration, dialysis." The shatkarmas (dhauti, basti, nauli) and yogic diet both rest on this physiology.

## 1. The alimentary canal

Total length ~9 m (in the cadaver). Wall layers (from inside): **mucosa**, **submucosa** (Meissner's plexus), **muscularis** (inner circular, outer longitudinal; **Auerbach's myenteric plexus** between them), **serosa**. The gut's own nervous system (enteric nervous system) works even without the brain.

| Part | Length / size | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | — | teeth (adult 32: 2-1-2-3 per quadrant; baby 20), tongue, **salivary glands**; mechanical chewing + **salivary amylase (ptyalin)** starts starch digestion |
| Pharynx | ~12 cm | common path for food and air; swallowing (deglutition) |
| **Oesophagus** | ~25 cm | **peristalsis**; upper third skeletal muscle, lower smooth; lower oesophageal sphincter |
| **Stomach** | J-shaped; ~1–1.5 L | cardia, fundus, body, pylorus; churning; **HCl** and **pepsin**; pyloric sphincter |
| **Small intestine** | ~6 m | **duodenum** (~25 cm, C-shaped; receives bile and pancreatic juice), **jejunum** (~2.5 m), **ileum** (~3.5 m; Peyer's patches; ileo-caecal valve) — **main site of digestion and absorption**: villi and microvilli raise the surface to ~200 m² |
| **Large intestine** | ~1.5 m | **caecum** (with the **vermiform appendix**) → **colon** (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid) → **rectum** → **anal canal**; absorbs water and salts; bacteria make **vitamin K** and some B vitamins |

- Parts of the **small intestine**: duodenum, **jejunum** and **ileum** (caecum and colon are large intestine). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024JUN-P2-Q102]]
- Order of the **large intestine**: **caecum → colon → rectum → anal canal**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q55]]
- The **vermiform appendix** is an outgrowth of the **caecum** (~9 cm; lymphoid tissue; appendicitis). [[PYQ: YOGA-2020JUN-P2-Q084]]

**Digestive steps in order**: **ingestion** (taking food in) → **digestion** (mechanical and chemical breakdown) → **absorption** (into blood and lymph) → **assimilation** (use by the cells) → **defecation / egestion**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2023JUN-P2-Q40]] Dhauti kriyā cleans the upper tract and improves these steps.

**GI motility** [[LIKELY]]: **peristalsis** (propulsive waves), **segmentation** (mixing contractions in the small intestine), **mass movements** in the colon (often after meals: **gastrocolic reflex**), **migrating motor complex** (housekeeping waves between meals, driven by **motilin**), and the **defecation reflex**. **Vomiting** centre in the medulla.

## 2. Digestive glands and secretions

**Salivary glands** (1–1.5 L saliva a day; pH ~6.8):

| Gland | Duct | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Parotid** (largest; in front of the ear) | **Stensen's (Stenson's) duct**, opens opposite the upper second molar | serous; mumps affects it |
| **Submandibular** | **Wharton's duct** | mixed; ~70% of saliva |
| **Sublingual** (smallest) | ducts of Rivinus / Bartholin's duct | mostly mucous |

[[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q085]]

**Liver** — the **largest gland** in the body (~1.5 kg; right upper abdomen). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P2-Q11]] (The skin is the largest organ overall.) Functions: makes **bile** (500–1,000 mL/day; **bile salts emulsify fats**; bile has no digestive enzymes) stored and concentrated in the **gall bladder**; stores **glycogen**, vitamins A, D, B12, iron; **gluconeogenesis**; makes **plasma proteins** (albumin, clotting factors, fibrinogen); **detoxification** (drugs, alcohol, ammonia → **urea**); breaks down old RBCs' haem → **bilirubin**; heat production. Lobules with hepatocytes; Kupffer cells (macrophages).

**Jaundice** (icterus; पीलिया) — **yellowish discoloration of skin, sclera and mucous membranes** because of an **increased level of serum bilirubin** (normal 0.3–1.2 mg/dL; visible above ~2–3 mg/dL). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q44]] Types: **pre-hepatic** (haemolytic: too much RBC breakdown), **hepatic** (hepatitis, cirrhosis), **post-hepatic** (obstructive: gallstones, tumour blocking the bile duct; pale stools, dark urine). [[LIKELY]]

**Stomach** — gastric juice ~2 L/day, pH 1.5–3.5:
- **Parietal (oxyntic) cells**: **HCl** (kills microbes, activates pepsin) and **intrinsic factor** (needed to absorb vitamin B12);
- **Chief (peptic) cells**: **pepsinogen** → pepsin (protein digestion); gastric lipase;
- **G cells**: **gastrin**; mucous cells: protective mucus and bicarbonate.

**Pancreas** — exocrine part: ~1.5 L/day of alkaline juice (pH ~8) with **trypsinogen** (activated to trypsin by enterokinase), chymotrypsinogen, **pancreatic amylase**, **lipase** (main fat-digesting enzyme), nucleases, and bicarbonate (neutralises acid chyme).

**Intestinal juice** (succus entericus): brush-border enzymes — maltase, sucrase, **lactase** (its lack = lactose intolerance), peptidases, enterokinase.

**End products of digestion**: carbohydrates → **monosaccharides** (glucose, fructose, galactose); proteins → **amino acids**; fats → **fatty acids and monoglycerides** (absorbed into lacteals as chylomicrons → lymph). Special absorption sites: **iron and calcium** in the duodenum; **vitamin B12 and bile salts** in the terminal ileum; water mostly in the small intestine, the rest in the colon.

**GI hormones** [[LIKELY]]:

| Hormone | From | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Gastrin** | G cells, stomach (antrum) | ↑ HCl and gastric motility |
| **Secretin** — the **first hormone discovered** (Bayliss and Starling, 1902) | S cells, duodenum (acid stimulus) | ↑ **bicarbonate-rich** pancreatic juice; ↓ gastric acid |
| **Cholecystokinin (CCK)** | I cells, duodenum (fat, protein) | **gall bladder contraction**, ↑ pancreatic enzymes, satiety |
| GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic peptide) | K cells, duodenum | ↑ insulin; ↓ gastric activity |
| **Motilin** | duodenum | migrating motor complex |
| **Ghrelin** | stomach | **hunger** hormone (leptin from fat is the satiety hormone) |
| Somatostatin | D cells | inhibits most GI secretions |

## 3. Digestive disorders

- **Dysphagia** — difficulty in swallowing. [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-Q40]] (Compare dyspepsia = indigestion; odynophagia = painful swallowing.)
- **Peptic ulcer** — gastric or **duodenal**. A **duodenal ulcer** occurs in the wall of the **first part of the small intestine**; it is the more common type (pain eased by food, felt 2–3 h after meals). Main causes: ***Helicobacter pylori*** infection (Marshall and Warren, Nobel 2005), NSAIDs, smoking; stress aggravates. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024DEC-P2-Q079]]
- **Indigestion** — major causes named in yoga-therapy texts: a diet deficient in **mineral salts**, **mental and emotional disturbances** (worry reduces digestive secretions), and the **habit of swallowing air and gluttony** (overeating). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q108]]
- Others [[LIKELY]]: **GERD** / acid reflux (hyperacidity), **constipation**, **irritable bowel syndrome** (gut–brain axis; stress-sensitive), haemorrhoids (piles: swollen veins of the anal canal), hepatitis, gallstones, lactose intolerance, ulcerative colitis.

## 4. The excretory system

**Excretory organs**: kidneys (urea, water, salts), lungs (CO₂, water), skin (sweat), liver (bile pigments), large intestine.

**Urinary tract in order**: **kidney → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019JUN-P2-Q50]]
- **Kidneys**: bean-shaped, ~150 g, ~11 cm, retroperitoneal at T12–L3 (right a little lower); outer **cortex**, inner **medulla** (renal pyramids), **pelvis**. Receive ~**1.2 L of blood a minute** (~20–25% of cardiac output).
- **Ureters**: ~25–30 cm, peristalsis carries urine.
- **Bladder**: holds ~400–600 mL; the urge comes at ~300–400 mL; detrusor muscle; internal (involuntary) and external (voluntary) sphincters.
- **Urethra**: ~4 cm in women, ~18–20 cm in men.

**Functions of the kidney** [[LIKELY]]: excretion of wastes (**urea**, uric acid, creatinine); water and electrolyte balance; **acid–base balance**; **BP regulation** (renin); **erythropoietin** (RBC production); **activation of vitamin D** (calcitriol); gluconeogenesis in starvation.

## 5. The nephron and renal physiology

**Nephron** — the structural and functional unit of the kidney; ~1 million in each kidney. Cortical nephrons (~85%, short loops) and juxtamedullary nephrons (long loops into the medulla: concentrate urine).

**Parts in order**: **glomerulus** (capillary tuft) inside **Bowman's capsule** (together: renal corpuscle / Malpighian body) → **proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)** → **loop of Henle** (descending and ascending limbs) → **distal convoluted tubule (DCT)** → **collecting duct**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q131]]

**Blood flow**: renal artery → … → **afferent arteriole** → glomerulus → **efferent arteriole** (narrower, keeps glomerular pressure high) → peritubular capillaries and **vasa recta** → renal vein. Combining blood and filtrate: **afferent arteriole → efferent arteriole → PCT → loop of Henle → collecting duct**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024JUN-P2-Q122]]

**Three processes of urine formation:**
1. **Glomerular filtration** — plasma minus proteins and cells filtered into Bowman's capsule. **GFR ≈ 125 mL/min ≈ 180 L/day**.
2. **Tubular reabsorption** — ~**99%** of the filtrate is reabsorbed: all glucose and amino acids (in the PCT; glucose spills into urine above the renal threshold ~180 mg/dL, as in diabetes), ~65% of Na⁺ and water in the PCT, more in the loop and DCT. Urine output only ~**1–1.5 L/day**.
3. **Tubular secretion** — H⁺, K⁺, creatinine, drugs added to the filtrate.

**Regulation of GFR** — **autoregulation** (myogenic response; **tubuloglomerular feedback** via the **macula densa** of the juxtaglomerular apparatus) and hormones: **angiotensin II** (constricts the efferent arteriole, keeping GFR up when BP falls) and **atrial natriuretic peptide** (dilates the afferent arteriole, raising GFR and Na⁺ loss). [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q074]] Sympathetic stimulation lowers GFR.

**Concentration of urine** [[LIKELY]] — the **countercurrent multiplier** (loop of Henle: descending limb permeable to water, ascending limb pumps out Na⁺/Cl⁻ but is impermeable to water) builds a salty medulla; the **vasa recta** act as the **countercurrent exchanger**; **ADH** inserts **aquaporin-2** channels in the collecting duct so water leaves into the medulla (concentrated urine, up to ~1,200 mOsm/L). **Aldosterone** makes the DCT and collecting duct reabsorb Na⁺ and secrete K⁺.

**Dialysis** [[LIKELY]] — when the kidneys fail (end-stage renal disease), wastes are removed artificially by **diffusion across a semipermeable membrane**: **haemodialysis** (blood passes through an artificial kidney — first built by Willem Kolff, 1943–45) or **peritoneal dialysis** (the peritoneum serves as the membrane). Kidney transplantation is the definitive treatment. Tests of kidney function: serum **creatinine**, blood urea, eGFR, urine protein.

**Terms**: polyuria (excess urine), oliguria (< 400 mL/day), anuria (< 100 mL/day), **diuresis**, micturition (urination reflex: sacral spinal cord, voluntary control from the cortex), uraemia, kidney stones (calcium oxalate most common).

# Endocrine System, Stress Hormones and Reproduction

> **At a glance** — secretions of endocrine glands = **hormones** · pancreas = **both exocrine and endocrine**; α-cells → **glucagon**, β → insulin, δ → somatostatin · pituitary → ACTH, TSH, GH, FSH, LH, prolactin; posterior → **vasopressin (ADH)**, oxytocin · hypothalamus → **CRH** and other releasing hormones · adrenal cortex → **glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids, gonadocorticoids**; medulla → **epinephrine** · stress hormones: **cortisol, adrenaline** · thymus → **T cells** · progesterone ← ovary · T3 ← thyroid · excess GH in adults → **acromegaly** · excess thyroid hormone → cardiac damage · LH → **ovulation**

Syllabus: "Endocrinology" and "Nervous system and glands: endocrine and exocrine glands", plus "Reproductive system". Endocrine questions are among the most frequent in Unit 6 (22 so far).

## 1. Glands and hormones

**Exocrine glands** pour their secretion through **ducts** onto a surface or into a cavity: salivary, sweat, sebaceous, lacrimal, mammary, gastric glands, and the **liver** (bile through the bile duct). **Endocrine glands** are **ductless**; they release **hormones** straight into the blood. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q33]]

- Exocrine among pituitary, liver, salivary gland, thyroid: **liver and salivary glands**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q34]]
- A gland that is **both exocrine and endocrine** (heterocrine): the **pancreas** (digestive enzymes through the pancreatic duct; insulin and glucagon into the blood). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q21]] The gonads are also mixed (gametes + sex hormones).

**Hormone** (Greek *hormaein*, to excite; term by Ernest Starling, 1905, after he and William Bayliss discovered **secretin** in 1902): a chemical messenger carried by the blood to target cells with matching receptors.

**Chemical kinds** [[LIKELY]]: **peptides/proteins** (insulin, GH, ADH; act on surface receptors via second messengers such as cAMP), **steroids** (cortisol, aldosterone, sex hormones; made from cholesterol; enter the cell and act on genes), **amines** (adrenaline, thyroid hormones, melatonin). Control mostly by **negative feedback**.

**Position of the glands from top to bottom**: pineal and **pituitary** (head) → **thyroid** and parathyroids (neck) → thymus (chest) → **adrenals** (on top of the kidneys) → pancreas → **gonads** (pelvis/scrotum). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q58]]

## 2. The endocrine glands

| Gland | Hormone(s) | Main action | Too little | Too much |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hypothalamus** | releasing and inhibiting hormones: **CRH**, TRH, GnRH, GHRH, somatostatin, dopamine (prolactin-inhibiting); makes ADH and oxytocin | controls the anterior pituitary; link between nervous and endocrine systems | | |
| **Anterior pituitary** (adenohypophysis) — "master gland", in the sella turcica of the sphenoid | **GH**, **TSH**, **ACTH**, **FSH**, **LH**, **prolactin** (+ MSH) | growth; thyroid, adrenal cortex and gonad control; milk production | pituitary dwarfism | **gigantism** (child), **acromegaly** (adult) |
| **Posterior pituitary** (neurohypophysis) — stores hypothalamic hormones | **vasopressin (ADH)**, **oxytocin** | ADH: water reabsorption in the collecting ducts; oxytocin: uterine contraction, milk ejection, bonding | **diabetes insipidus** | SIADH |
| **Pineal** | **melatonin** | sleep–wake, circadian rhythm | | |
| **Thyroid** (largest purely endocrine gland) | **T4 (thyroxine)**, **T3 (tri-iodothyronine)**; **calcitonin** (C cells) | metabolic rate, growth, brain development; calcitonin lowers blood Ca²⁺ | **cretinism** (child), **myxoedema** (adult), goitre (iodine lack) | **Graves' disease**, thyrotoxicosis (exophthalmos, tachycardia) |
| **Parathyroids** (4) | **PTH** (parathormone) | raises blood Ca²⁺ | **tetany** | bone loss, kidney stones |
| **Thymus** | **thymosin** | maturation of **T lymphocytes** | | |
| **Adrenal cortex** | **mineralocorticoids** (aldosterone: zona glomerulosa), **glucocorticoids** (cortisol: zona fasciculata), **gonadocorticoids** (androgens: zona reticularis) — "salt, sugar, sex" | Na⁺/water retention; stress, blood sugar, anti-inflammation; sex characteristics | **Addison's disease** | **Cushing's syndrome** (cortisol), **Conn's syndrome** (aldosterone) |
| **Adrenal medulla** | **adrenaline (epinephrine)** ~80%, noradrenaline | fight-or-flight | | phaeochromocytoma (hypertension) |
| **Pancreas** — islets of Langerhans (~1–2% of pancreas) | **α-cells: glucagon**; **β-cells: insulin**; **δ-cells: somatostatin**; PP cells | glucagon raises, insulin lowers blood glucose | **diabetes mellitus** | hypoglycaemia |
| **Ovaries** | **oestrogen** (follicles), **progesterone** (corpus luteum), inhibin, relaxin | female characteristics, menstrual cycle, pregnancy | | |
| **Testes** | **testosterone** (Leydig cells), inhibin | male characteristics, sperm production | | |
| Others | kidney (renin, erythropoietin, calcitriol), heart (**ANP**), stomach (gastrin, ghrelin), intestine (secretin, CCK), fat (leptin), placenta (hCG) | | | |

**Questions on these:**
- Hormones of the **pituitary**: **ACTH** and **TSH** (epinephrine and cortisol are adrenal; T3 thyroid). [[PYQ: YOGA-2022DEC-P2-Q54]]
- A hormone of the **hypothalamus**: **corticotrophin-releasing hormone (CRH)**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q112]]
- From the **adrenal cortex**: **glucocorticoids, gonadocorticoids and mineralocorticoids** (melatonin is pineal, prolactin pituitary). [[PYQ: YOGA-2024SEP-P2-Q121]]
- Hormones of the **pancreas**: **somatostatin and glucagon** (with insulin). [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q34]]
- **α-cells** of the pancreas secrete **glucagon**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2019DEC-P2-Q092]]
- **Pancreas → insulin; thyroid → thyroxine; pituitary → vasopressin; adrenal → corticoids.** [[PYQ: YOGA-2017NOV-P3-Q66]]
- **Adrenal medulla → epinephrine; pituitary → oxytocin; adrenal cortex → cortisol; ovaries → oestrogen.** [[PYQ: YOGA-2025JUN-P2-Q051]]
- **Progesterone** is secreted by the **ovary** (corpus luteum; later the placenta). [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q24]]
- **T3** is secreted by the **thyroid**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018JUL-P2-Q25]]
- **Thymus** hormones help the multiplication and maturation of **T cells**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2018DEC-P2-Q25]]
- **Acromegaly** is caused by excess **growth hormone** in adults (enlarged hands, feet, jaw). [[PYQ: YOGA-2021JUN-P2-S19]]
- High levels of **thyroid hormones** damage the heart (tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, heart failure: "thyrotoxic heart disease"). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q080]]
- The **hypothalamus** (limbic system) is the centre of emotional and instinctive sexual behaviour, acting through its **releasing hormones** (GnRH → LH and FSH → sex hormones). [[PYQ: YOGA-2023DEC-P2-Q079]]

**Diabetes mellitus** — type 1 (autoimmune loss of β-cells; insulin needed), type 2 (insulin **resistance**; lifestyle, obesity; ~90%), gestational. Classic triad: **polyuria** (frequent urination), **polydipsia** (increased thirst), **polyphagia** (increased hunger). Diagnosis: fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥ 6.5%. Matching asked: **polydipsia → increased thirst; polyuria → frequent urination; polyphagia → increased hunger; haemorrhoids → swollen veins** (piles). [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q124]]

[[LIKELY]] Diabetes insipidus vs mellitus; cretinism vs myxoedema; Cushing's vs Addison's; hormone of the parathyroid; which gland is called the master gland.

## 3. Stress physiology

**Stress hormones** — the main ones are **cortisol** (glucocorticoid) and **epinephrine (adrenaline)**; glucagon, growth hormone and prolactin also rise but are not called stress hormones in the textbooks. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P2-Q28]]

**Fight-or-flight** (Walter Cannon) is driven by **adrenaline** from the adrenal medulla under sympathetic command. [[PYQ: YOGA-2017JAN-P3-Q14]]

**Two stress axes:**

| | **SAM axis** (sympathetic–adrenal–medullary) | **HPA axis** (hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | seconds | minutes to hours |
| Pathway | hypothalamus → sympathetic nerves → **adrenal medulla** | hypothalamus (**CRH**) → anterior pituitary (**ACTH**) → **adrenal cortex** (**cortisol**) |
| Effects | heart rate, BP, blood sugar ↑; pupils dilate; airways open; digestion stops | blood glucose ↑ (gluconeogenesis), protein breakdown, immunity and inflammation ↓, memory affected |
| Long-term harm | hypertension, heart disease | diabetes, obesity (abdominal), infections, depression, osteoporosis |

Selye's **general adaptation syndrome** (alarm → resistance → exhaustion) describes the course of this response (see *Mental Health*).

**Yoga, pain and relaxation** — yoga nidrā relieves pain by releasing the brain's own opioids, **endorphins and enkephalins**. [[PYQ: YOGA-2022JUN-P2-Q106]] Research on yoga also reports lower cortisol and catecholamines, more GABA, and higher melatonin. [[LIKELY]]

## 4. Reproductive system

**Male**: **testes** in the scrotum (2–3 °C below body temperature for sperm production) — seminiferous tubules (spermatogenesis, ~64–74 days; **Sertoli cells** nurse sperm), **Leydig (interstitial) cells** (testosterone); epididymis (sperm mature and are stored) → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra; accessory glands: seminal vesicles (fructose-rich fluid), **prostate**, bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands.

**Female**: **ovaries** (oogenesis; follicles; ~1–2 million oocytes at birth, ~400 ovulated in life) → **fallopian tubes** (fimbriae, ampulla — the usual site of **fertilisation**) → **uterus** (endometrium, myometrium; implantation ~day 6–7 after fertilisation) → cervix → vagina.

**Hormonal control**: hypothalamus **GnRH** → anterior pituitary **FSH** and **LH** → gonads.
- **FSH**: follicle growth (female); spermatogenesis via Sertoli cells (male).
- **LH**: the mid-cycle **LH surge triggers ovulation** and forms the corpus luteum (female) [[PYQ: YOGA-2024JUN-P2-Q104]]; stimulates Leydig cells to make testosterone (male; also called ICSH).

**Menstrual cycle** (~28 days) [[LIKELY]]:

| Phase | Days | Ovary | Hormones | Uterus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Menstrual | 1–5 | new follicles start | low oestrogen and progesterone | endometrium shed |
| Follicular / proliferative | 6–13 | Graafian follicle grows | **oestrogen** rises | endometrium rebuilds |
| **Ovulation** | ~14 | follicle ruptures, ovum released | **LH surge** (with FSH peak) | — |
| Luteal / secretory | 15–28 | **corpus luteum** | **progesterone** (and oestrogen) | glands secrete; ready for implantation |

If no pregnancy, the corpus luteum degenerates (corpus albicans) and progesterone falls → menstruation. If pregnancy occurs, **hCG** from the placenta keeps the corpus luteum alive (basis of pregnancy tests). **Menarche** ~12–13 years; **menopause** ~45–55 years (oestrogen falls; hot flushes, bone loss).

**Yoga and reproductive health** — yoga for dysmenorrhoea, PCOS (insulin resistance, androgen excess), menopausal symptoms, and pregnancy care are common Unit 7–8 topics; mūla bandha and aśvinī mudrā act on the pelvic floor.
